# Prompt set for chapter illustrations

Generation mode: built-in ImageGen.

## Shared art direction

Wide 16:9 editorial historical illustration for a serious Russian nonfiction book about economic history. Cinematic painterly realism, textured gouache and oil-brush surfaces, muted earth palette, charcoal shadows, one restrained amber accent, layered depth, natural human faces and gestures, historically grounded, no fantasy, no text, no numbers, no logos, no recognizable brands, no border, no watermark. Leave some calm negative space near the upper left for a chapter title.

## Chapter prompts

1. **Hunter-gatherers:** Late-Paleolithic camp at dawn beside a river valley; cooperative division of plants, fish, hides and stone tools; one traveler leaving on a long-distance exchange route; reciprocal sharing, mobility and ecological limits.
2. **Neolithic Revolution:** Early farming village at harvest; communal grain storage, threshing, goats, irrigation and mud-brick homes; shared work contrasted with the first guarded surplus.
3. **Temple economies:** Ancient Mesopotamian canal city; cuneiform scribes, barley measures, public granary, unloading boats and stepped temple; useful centralized accounting together with hierarchy.
4. **Metals, trade and money:** Bronze-to-Iron Age Mediterranean harbor where caravan and ships meet; metal ingots, scales, smiths and early stamped coins; widening trust and fragile trade networks.
5. **Greece and Rome:** Roman port and civic marketplace; grain ships, artisans, aqueduct and forum; people in unequal legal and labor roles; infrastructure and trade alongside imperial cost.
6. **Beyond Europe:** Song-dynasty Chinese canal city; barges, lock gate, iron workshop, paper records, coin counting and distant Asian merchants; sophisticated institutions independent of Europe.
7. **Feudal Europe:** Medieval manor in autumn; common fields, watermill, toll bridge, castle and cathedral workshop; customary cooperation, dues and heavy obligations.
8. **Commercial Revolution:** Late-fifteenth-century Atlantic port; caravels, ledgers, maps, barrels and silver scales; opportunity, coercion and colonial extraction without heroic conquest imagery.
9. **Institutions and science:** Early-eighteenth-century city street joining a printing shop, coffeehouse, experimental workshop and exchange courtyard; reproducible knowledge and scalable trust.
10. **First Industrial Revolution:** Early-nineteenth-century British textile mill; steam, water power, looms, coal carts and worker housing; productivity and exhausting factory discipline.
11. **Second Industrial Revolution:** Circa-1905 electrified industrial city and freight terminal; steel, power lines, telegraph, assembly production, railway and steamship; scale and monopoly power.
12. **1914–1945:** Restrained reconstruction-era railway station; damaged factories, ration baskets, displaced families, bread queue and locomotive repair; war, depression and institutional collapse without graphic violence.
13. **Planned economies:** Mid-century Soviet-style factory and planning mezzanine; engineers, physical charts, telephones, turbines, housing blocks and a shop queue; mobilization power and information bottlenecks.
14. **Golden Age:** Mixed-economy city edge, 1945–1973; factory shift change, apartments, school, clinic, buses, family cars, union hall and park; broad prosperity with pollution at the margins.
15. **Turning point:** Late-twentieth-century container port beside a deindustrializing city; closed factory, job seekers, financial towers and oil tanker; globalization and the shift toward finance.
16. **Digital economy:** Early-2000s software office linked to server room and container logistics hub; programmers, call-center workers, warehouse staff and online sellers; innovation, concentration and financial fragility.
17. **World 2010–2026:** Contemporary global neighborhood; remote work, delivery riders, AI lab, automated warehouse, clinic, solar panels and flood barriers; inequality, climate stress, pandemic memory and mutual aid.
18. **Recurring patterns:** A realistic river of surplus passing through irrigation, workshop, railway, power grid and digital network; toll gates and hoards constrict one bank while schools, clinics and open workshops expand the other.
19. **Bridge to the model:** Plausible near-future civic economy; open neighborhood workshop, transparent public-project ledger, automated fabrication, community energy, clinic, childcare and restored wetland; common infrastructure, auditable digital payments and social dividend.
