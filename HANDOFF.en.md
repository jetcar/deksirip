# HANDOFF — Decentralized Social Economy Concept

## Purpose

Develop and rigorously criticize a model of society with social ownership, decentralized economic and political decisions, and high productivity and automation.

This is not a proven theory. The goal is to find fundamental weaknesses, counterexamples, historical analogues, and ways to test the assumptions.

## Core model

Means of production belong to society. Every citizen receives a social dividend; unused personal resources may accumulate, but accumulation does not grant ownership of factories, land, or other means of production. Citizens may voluntarily direct their shares or labour to scientific, technological, infrastructure, cultural, and other projects.

Ordinary production follows demand, available resources, and people's willingness to work on the relevant tasks. There is no central body setting physical quotas in advance. Long-term projects use staged, transparent, voluntary funding. Strategic reserves are proposed and approved by citizens; experts and AI provide analysis but do not make final decisions.

Every allocation should be reproducible through the chain: citizens' decision → public rule → source data → calculation → result. Citizens may make mistakes; the system should make consequences visible, preserve reversibility where possible, and allow decisions to be changed.

## Education and AI

Citizens need statistical literacy, probability, causal reasoning, source checking, uncertainty awareness, and the ability to recognize manipulation. AI is a personal analytical tool: it can check claims, find sources, model consequences, compare historical cases, detect contradictions, and explain expertise. It has no final decision rights or independent control over resources. Competing independent models are preferable to one information authority.

## Army and external world

The army is voluntary and professional, with a limited mandate to respond immediately to direct armed attack. It has civilian control, transparent funding, no independent economic base, political neutrality, and accountability for abuse. The economy is not isolated: foreign trade, imports, exports, and technology exchange are possible, while domestic means of production remain socially owned.

## Difference from centralized socialism

The model does not use “state planner → resource allocation.” It uses “citizens → individual decisions → aggregate allocation.” Experts advise, AI analyses, infrastructure executes, and citizens set goals.

## Historical list of open questions

The original list below is preserved as a historical record. Many items were later addressed by explicit principles. The current list is in [critique/26-current-reassessment.en.md](critique/26-current-reassessment.en.md).

1. How is the social dividend calculated?
2. How are personal units connected to scarce physical resources?
3. How are inflation, scarcity, and false price signals handled?
4. Can accumulated personal resources recreate unequal influence?
5. How are public goods funded when few people want to fund them personally?
6. How is basic research funded?
7. How are projects with 20–50 year horizons supported?
8. How are strategic reserves formed without a planning body?
9. How are marketing, propaganda, and attention concentration handled?
10. Can popularity systematically defeat usefulness?
11. How is AI or digital infrastructure prevented from becoming a power center?
12. How is a military coup prevented?
13. How is an organized minority handled when the majority is passive?
14. How is new bureaucracy prevented?
15. How are unpleasant, dangerous, and unpopular tasks performed?
16. How does the system behave at low productivity?
17. How does it behave during war, epidemics, or major disasters?
18. How does it interact with the capitalist world without abandoning its ownership principles?
19. What happens if most citizens do not want regular participation in governance?
20. Which historical or modern systems implemented parts of this model?

## How to criticize the model

Do not stop at “people are greedy,” “people are stupid,” “communism was already tried,” or “this is utopian.” Give a causal mechanism:

`X → creates incentive Y → participants rationally do Z → the system develops problem W.`

The most valuable objections are those that cannot be removed by changing one parameter or adding one simple rule. Look for real data and sources rather than invented historical facts, and distinguish fundamental, institutional, technical, and experimental questions.
