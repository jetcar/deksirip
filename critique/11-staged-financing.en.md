# Part 11. Tranches instead of one-time financing

Objection: withdrawal of funding is not necessary. Nobody gives away 50 billion at once - this makes sense only if you immediately buy a lithographic machine. As in the current business, money is added as needed and according to reports.

Answer: you described the current mechanism correctly, doubting your own understanding is unjustified. And this objection **does fix** the coalition asymmetry from file 10. But it shifts the load to another node, and this node is the weakest in the design.

---

# I. How it works now (since you asked)

Compact, because further it is needed for comparison.

**Venture.** Financing by rounds: seed, A, B, C. Each round is a new valuation of the company and new money for specific achievements (“release a product”, “reach 10 thousand paying users”, “complete the second phase of testing”). The investor receives a seat on the board of directors and the right to veto key decisions. Between rounds there is regular reporting, usually monthly.

A key element that is usually overlooked: **The main signal is not the report, but the ability to attract a new outside investor.** The round is led by a “lead” - someone who has not participated before, evaluates the company with a fresh look and puts his money. If a new lead isn't found and only existing investors close the round, it's called an insider round and reads as a red flag precisely because the confirmation is coming from people already tied up in the past decision. A down round is a public verdict that a previous estimate was incorrect.

**Corporate loan.** Credit line with the right to withdraw in parts, covenants (obligations to comply with financial indicators), violation of the covenant gives the lender the right to demand repayment early. That is, the same principle: money comes in portions, each portion is determined by the state of affairs.

**Science.** It's **different** here, and that's important. Grants are given for 3–5 years under a work plan, but control is not based on the achievement of results - we do not guarantee results in science by definition - but on the quality of the process, publications and reputation of the team. Evaluated by fellow specialists. Interim milestones are soft, and withdrawal of a grant mid-term is rare. This is a conscious rejection of strict trench control, because strict control in science selects the predictable, not the important.

**Government Projects.** Annual appropriations plus multi-year commitments. Large infrastructure - stages with acceptance.

**Why does all this exist?** Tranching is a solution to the agency problem: the investor cannot check the intentions and abilities of the founder in advance, so he gives money in portions and buys the right to change his mind. The literature on this topic begins with Gompers' 1995 paper on optimal tranching of venture capital investments.

#II. What does your objection really fix?

In File 10, I argued that stopping requires the atomized mass to **actively withdraw** funding against a concentrated group that wants to continue, and according to Olson, the concentrated one wins.

For tranches, **default is reversed**. Money doesn't flow if no one has performed the action. Continuation requires activity, stopping happens on its own. Now the already concentrated group has to re-convince the dispersed one every period, and not vice versa.

This is the same technique as in other areas: the expiration of the law by default (sunset clause), a budget from scratch instead of a budget from what has been achieved. The reception is good, and it really removes my argument in its original formulation. Counted.

#III. What trenching doesn't fix

## 1. The dilemma of silence is new and, in my opinion, the most acute

Tranching only helps if the default is “stop” **and** someone competently reviews the decision. Let's look at both possible defaults.

**The default is “continue”** (for example, a citizen once set up regular deductions for 5 years - the concept directly allows for this): we are back exactly to the original problem. Inertia plus a concentrated continuation coalition.

**Default “stop”**: then each project must re-conquer attention every quarter. But attention is a scarce resource, and the vast majority of projects simply won't be revised on time. Projects survive not because they are good, but because they are **memorable**. The one who best reminds of himself gets funding.

And this hits **hardest of all on long-term projects**: a twenty-year project must wage a twenty-year campaign to retain attention, despite the fact that it has nothing to show until the very end. Tranching, while saving from inertia, worsens the situation of fundamental science - that is, the area for which a long-term financing mechanism was introduced into the concept.

**Neither of the two defaults gives a good result.** This is not configurable: any intermediate solution (extend automatically, but with periodic confirmation) is a choice of a point on the segment between two bad ends.

## 2. Someone should read reports

Report tranching works in venture because the report is read by a member of the board of directors who has money on the line, and he asks awkward questions. Tranching is a **procedure**, and its information content entirely depends on the presence of a monitor.

The concept of the monitor is not by design (problem M3). Forty million people, each with 250 units, do not read the progress report on the development of a lithographic installation. The decision “whether to give the next tranche” degrades to “did I come across something bad about this project in the feed.”

Tranching does not create competent assessment - it creates **more moments in which competent assessment is required**. If there is no assessment, you have multiplied the number of incompetent solutions rather than improving their quality.

## 3. Arithmetic of attention - and it can be counted

Your own remark unfolds into a specific test. A 10 billion project with a 12-year horizon and quarterly tranches is not one solution, but about 48 solutions. Multiply by the number of projects the citizen funds.

Even with a modest 20 projects per person and quarterly review, that works out to 80 technical report reviews per year. This is not “half an hour on Sunday”, this is a part-time job on a permanent basis, which in addition requires various special training for the engine, the vaccine and the power grid.

What does a rational person do? **Delegates.** And we come to M4 - delegation leads to a sedate concentration of curators. Tranching does not eliminate this problem, it **increases the workload that creates it**: the more often the revision, the greater the pressure to delegate.

This, in my opinion, is the main point: your correction is correct and shifts the load from the mechanism that I criticized to the mechanism that was weaker to begin with.

## 4. No external evaluator with fresh eyes

Information in a venture round comes not from the report, but from the willingness of the **new** investor to put money down. The value lies precisely in the fact that he is not bound by a past decision and therefore is not subject to escalation of obligations.

In your system, all funders are by design insiders: they have already invested, their choice is recorded in a transparent ledger forever. There is no external party to assign a new assessment. You get a form of trench financing without the element that makes it informative. In the language of venture, this is an endless series of insider rounds, and this is precisely read as a sign of trouble.

## 5. Refusal of a tranche is not a loss event

In a venture, refusal to give the next tranche means that the company dies and previous investments are written off at a loss. The investor therefore thinks seriously - the decision hurts himself.

Your refusal means that your 250 units will go somewhere else next month. No loss event occurs. The decision remains free, which means it is taken carelessly - no matter how many times a year it is taken.

## 6. Milestones are not contractable everywhere

Milestone tranching works where milestones can be written down in advance and verified: release a product, recruit users, close a testing phase. It doesn't work well where your concept is aimed.

What is the seventh year milestone for high energy physics research? That is why science is funded by evaluating the process and people, and not by achieving the result. Your amendment imports a mechanism from an area where it works (venture with observable milestones) into an area where it has been deliberately abandoned.

---

#IV. Bottom line

**Counted:** tranching reverses the default and removes my asymmetry of coalitions in the original formulation. The objection is correct, the mechanism is described correctly.

**It remains:** tranching is a procedure for distributing decisions over time, and not a source of competence. It improves a system where there is a monitor, but does not improve a system where there is no monitor. In your case it gives:

- the dilemma of default, both ends of which are bad, and the “stop by default” end hits long-term projects the hardest;
- increase in the number of required decisions → increase in pressure to delegate → M4, concentration of curators;
- a form of tranche financing without an external appraiser, that is, an endless insider round;
- free refusal, due to which multiplying the number of solutions does not improve their quality.
