# Part 7. How each problem is solved now - and what the concept does with it

## Why this file

The objection “your system has problem X” proves nothing if X exists in the existing system. The real question is always: **what specific institution is holding X within acceptable limits today, at what cost, and what happens to that institution in your design.**

The analysis below shows that most criticism falls into three very different categories:

- **⛔ Strictly worse.** Today the problem is solved by a specific working mechanism; the concept removes it and **directly prohibits replacement**. This is the core of the criticism - it is almost impossible to argue here.
- **⚠️ Worse.** Today the solution is bad, but better than it will be. The debate is about degree, not principle.
- **≈ Parity.** Today it’s also not decided. It is useless as an argument against the concept, although this is often what is said most loudly.
- **✅ The concept is better.** The existing system solves poorly or at a price that the concept does not pay.

Result for 23 points: **7 strictly worse, 8 worse, 5 parity, 3 better.** Details below.

---

## 1. Allocation of capital between competing techniques

**Now.** The price of a capital good is formed by the fact that the buyer puts his own non-renewable stock against his own valuation. Wrong - lost. Profit and loss is not a morality, but a feedback signal. Plus the market for corporate control: an ineffective owner loses an asset.

**Price of this solution.** The signal is only accurate in terms of monetary return. Externalities are not taken into account by construction; short-term nature of public markets (pressure of quarterly reporting); bubbles are regular.

**In concept.** There is no residual contender. This means that the price of a capital good is either administered (a prohibited body) or noise.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse.** This is not “the signal has become weaker”, it is the absence of a signal in the absence of a substitute. Lange proposed a replacement (trial and error method of the central council), Roemer - monitor banks. You have neither one nor the other.

---

## 2. Monitoring management (agency problem)

**Now.** The Berle-Means problem is real and not completely resolved. But there are **five** parallel mechanisms: concentrated holders (PE, VC with board seats, family owners), the market for corporate control (hostile takeovers), statutory audit, covenanted lenders, and delegated oversight (boards of directors, regulators).

**Price.** Enron, Wirecard, Theranos - mechanisms regularly fail. Index funds are passive owners: the Big Three control about 20% of the S&P 500 voting power and monitor it weakly. Proxy advisors (ISS, Glass Lewis) are a duopoly that essentially delegates judgment to two private firms.

**In the concept.** All five mechanisms are absent: shares are not concentrated, are not sold, there are no takeovers, there are no creditors, the supervisory authority is prohibited.

**Verdict: ⛔ Strictly worse.** And note: passive index funds are already an experiment in “atomized ownership without monitoring,” and it has created exactly what the theory predicts: the delegation of judgment to a few private arbiters.

---

## 3. The rigidity of the budget constraint and the exit of ineffective

**Now.** Bankruptcy. An unsuccessful company is liquidated, resources are redistributed. A venture fund that loses money will not raise the next one.

**Price.** Restriction is severe **not for everyone**: 2008 (TARP), 2020, “too big to fall.” For large financial institutions, the budget constraint is soft, and this is a recognized failure.

**In concept.** The citizen risks the flow that will return; a failed project does not go bankrupt; re-application is possible.

**Verdict: ⚠️ worse, but not “strictly”.** An honest counter-argument from your opponent: “they also save you in capitalism.” Your answer should be about scale: now a soft restriction is an exception for dozens of systemically important organizations, while for you it is a rule for everyone. That's a difference of two orders of magnitude, but it's a debate about degree.

---

## 4. Who makes decisions in an operating enterprise

**Now.** Management hierarchy, legitimized by property rights, with feedback through P&L. Plus a working cooperative sector (Mondragon), where decisions are made by the collective - but with its own bank, central apparatus and the right to bankrupt its enterprises (Fagor, 2013).

**Price.** Workplace authoritarianism, alienation, is a real and valid objection on the left.

**In concept.** Not described at all. Three options, all with known outcomes: the work collective (Yugoslav pathologies), the appointed manager (prohibited body), no one.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse, and it's a hole, not a loss.** No response is not a neutral position; half the economic system is simply unspecified.

---

## 5. Concentration of power through delegation

**Now.** The concentration is enormous: the Big Three, proxy advisers, party apparatuses, editorial offices. No one is pretending this is resolved.

**Price/balances.** There are antitrust laws, fiduciary duties, mandatory disclosure, conflict of interest rules, competing jurisdictions, the ability to withdraw from the fund with one click.

**In concept.** Delegation is inevitable (lack of attention), concentration is sedate, but there are no counterbalances: there is no antimonopoly authority, there is no fiduciary responsibility, there is a way out of the “list of recommendations”, but there are few alternatives.

**Verdict: ⚠️ worse.** The problem is the same, there are no deterrence tools. A good question for your opponent: name a mechanism for limiting the concentration of delegates that is not a body.

---

## 6. Transforming economic inequality into political inequality

**Now.** Poorly resolved, and this is the main substantive complaint against capitalism. Lobbying in the United States is about $4 billion per year, the 2024 election cycle is about $15 billion. Gilens and Page (2014): the independent influence of the median citizen on politics is statistically indistinguishable from zero.

**Counterbalances.** Universal suffrage, independent courts, free press, progressive taxation, campaign finance restrictions.

**In concept.** Stock inequality eliminated. But economics **is** politics by construction: direct units = make a political decision. This means that any influence on the foreign direction of units is immediately political, and there is no division of spheres that can be defended.

**Verdict: ≈ parity, with a caveat.** You remove one channel (wealth → politics) and create another (attention → capital → everything). It is impossible to assert that the second is obviously worse than the first without a model. This is one of the few places where your design takes a hit - use it to prevent your opponents from wasting time.

---

## 7. Useful, but unpopular: sewerage, maintenance, supervision

**Now.** Three mechanisms: (a) tax - forced, not dependent on enthusiasm; (b) regulation—mandatory standards; (c) tariffs and utility monopolies with service obligations.

**Price.** The solution is mediocre even this way: the infrastructure deficit in the United States is estimated by ASCE at trillions of dollars of accumulated under-repairs, the overall condition rating is “C”. The political cycle systematically favors putting a ribbon on a new bridge over fixing an old one.

**In concept.** All three mechanisms are missing. Funding is voluntary and follows attention, but sewerage never attracts attention.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse.** Here is the fundamental difference: the tax works **precisely because** it does not require enthusiasm. Your system replaces coercion with enthusiasm in an area where there is no enthusiasm due to the nature of the task.

---

## 8. Market failures where there is no profit: antibiotics, rare diseases

**Now.** The market here **openly fails**: new classes of antibiotics are almost not being developed, large companies have left the field, because the correct use of a new antibiotic is as rare as possible. The solution is non-market: Orphan Drug Act (1983), bonuses and “pull” mechanisms, guaranteed purchases.

**In concept.** Formally, this class of problems is available to your system: citizens can decide to finance antibiotics. But in fact, the same logic of attention is at work as in philanthropy, where funding is correlated with the visibility of a disease rather than its burden.

**Verdict: ≈ parity, rather ⚠️.** Important: **this is the only large class of problems where the existing system recognizes its failure and cures it using exactly your method - a collective decision outside the market.** Hence a strong argument in your favor and a weak point of your opponent. But note: treatment is implemented by law and agency, not by voluntary attention.

---

## 9. Basic science

**Now.** Solved by **insulation from popularity**, not popularity: multi-year grants, endowment, tenure, peer review, basic level institutional funding. In the United States, basic research is predominantly government funded, while business dominates applied development—a separation that is intentional.

**Price.** Known pathologies: conservatism of peer review, low agreement between reviewers, NIH application success rate of about 20%, average age of first major grant about 43 years, systematic bias against risky ideas. The story of Katalin Kariko is a direct illustration of the failure of the existing system.

**In concept.** Funding based on the attention of citizens with recall at any time is the **opposite** of the principle of isolation. Empirics of science crowdfunding: median project - thousands of dollars, thematic bias towards charming objects.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse.** Your opponent is strong here, but you can also hit: the existing system is also bad, just bad in a different way. An honest formulation of the debate is “isolation from popularity with bureaucratic conservatism” versus “popularity without bureaucracy.” The former empirically produced all modern science; the second produced nothing comparable.

---

## 10. Projects with a horizon of decades

**Now.** Mechanisms for tying hands: interstate treaties (ITER), endowments with a statutory mandate, multi-year appropriations, regulated asset base of utility monopolies with a guaranteed return for 30-40 years, concessions, patent as a temporary monopoly.

**Price.** Short horizon of public markets; a political cycle of 4–5 years cuts long programs; Canceling projects when there is a change of government is common.

**In concept.** Explicit revocability of obligations. A 20-year project in its 7th year looks like a failure in design.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse.** Key asymmetry: today's mechanisms exist **precisely to** deprive the current majority of the opportunity to change their minds. Your system elevates the possibility of changing your mind to a principle. This is a direct contradiction to the goals.

---

## 11. Strategic reserves and rare disasters

**Now.** Specialized agencies with a statutory mandate and a protected budget: oil reserves, food and medical supplies, mandatory reserve standards.

**Price.** Doesn't work well. The American Strategic National Stockpile was financed by about $600 million a year before 2020; The supply of respirators used up in the 2009 H1N1 pandemic has never been replenished. Almost all states had plans for pandemics, but almost no one had adequate supplies.

**In concept.** Citizens decide, experts only advise.

**Verdict: ⚠️ worse - but this is your best chance in the argument.** The opponent’s “need a body” argument is weakened by the fact that the body **existed**, had a mandate and a budget, and failed. Your counter question: Why does a permanent body with concentrated responsibility systematically underfund rare risks, and what in your scheme makes it worse, rather than just different? The honest answer: your protected budget is disappearing - the same one that was small in 2019, but was not zero.

---

## 12. Manipulation, marketing, propaganda

**Now.** Global advertising spending is about $1 trillion a year, about 1% of global GDP. Counterbalances: the law on false advertising, mandatory disclosure for issuers of securities, responsibility for the prospectus, the institution of independent audit, and most importantly - **a professional skeptic who makes money from debunking** (short selling, investigative journalism, analysts).

**In concept.** Marketing competes directly for capital, not consumer demand. At the same time, there is no short position, there is no resale of the share, which means it is impossible to make money by exposing an overvalued project. The professional skeptic is absent as a class.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse.** This is the most underrated point. Hinderburg and Maddoff whistleblowers exist because there is money to be made from the truth. Take away the income and all that remains is belief. Forecast: the share of the social product spent on persuasion grows until the marginal return equalizes the production one.

---

## 13. AI as a center of power

**Now.** Not resolved to any extent. Recommendation algorithms already determine the information agenda of billions of people; regulation in its infancy; the concentration of computing power and models is high.

**In concept.** The AI ​​advisor becomes an economic decision-making infrastructure, which strengthens both the agenda problem and the correlation of judgments (the independence condition in Condorcet's theorem).

**Verdict: ≈ parity in fact, ⚠️ in trajectory.** The objection “AI will become power” hits both systems, so as an argument against you it is weak. The strong version of the objection is different and specific: your system **makes AI a mandatory part of the decision loop** when it is currently optional.

---

## 14. Army and civilian control

**Now.** Operates through specific positions of authority: Department of Defense with civilian leadership, parliamentary budget control, rotation of command, division of security forces, military justice, appointment and dismissal of high command.

**Price.** Doesn’t work everywhere and not always; the military-industrial complex as a political subject; mandate creep (AUMF).

**In concept.** All of the listed mechanisms require a professional apparatus, which has been cancelled. “Civil control” without a control body is not a mechanism, but a wish.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse.** Here is the key wording: civilian control over the army has never been exercised directly by citizens anywhere. It was always carried out by **another professional hierarchy**. You remove the second hierarchy and leave the first.

---

## 15. Capture of resources by organized minorities

**Now.** Bad. Industry lobbies, regulatory capture, professional licenses as a barrier, agricultural subsidies in rich countries are classic Olson illustrations.

**Counterbalances.** Judicial control, antitrust law, mandatory disclosure of lobbying, free press, competition between branches of government.

**In concept.** The asymmetry is the same, but the instrument is more direct: there is no need to buy a politician, it is enough to coordinate your own dividends. There are no counterweights.

**Verdict: ⚠️ worse.** The problem is common, but your capture costs are lower and your resistance is weaker.

---

## 16. Bureaucracy

**Now.** Huge, expensive, self-replicating. This is a legitimate complaint.

**The price of today's decision.** But it is **formalized**: there are job regulations, responsibility, an appeal procedure, audit, court, dismissal.

**In concept.** The apparatus is still necessary - registries, audit, anti-fraud, identification, warehouses, data standards. The difference is that he is not recognized and therefore not formally accountable.

**Verdict: ⚠️ worse.** Informal power is worse than formal power precisely because you cannot make a claim against it. "The Tyranny of Structurelessness" by Jo Freeman is an accurate statement, and it was written by people who sympathized with your intuition.

---

## 17. Physical resources and externalities

**Now.** Bad. Externalities are by definition outside of price; climate is the biggest example of this. Partial solutions: Pigouvian tax, cap-and-trade, regulations, inventories, mandatory emissions reporting.

**In concept.** No worse and no better: the same problem, the same missing mechanism. The declared natural accounting rests on the debate between Neurath and Mises, where computing power is not a bottleneck - the bottleneck is that the coefficients are endogenous to the choice of technology, and quality is multidimensional.

**Verdict: ≈ parity.** As an argument against you - weak. But it doesn’t work like your advantage either: the concept does not contain an internalization mechanism.

---

## 18. Inflation and macro balance

**Today.** An independent central bank with a statutory mandate, separated from immediate voting so politicians cannot print money before elections. There are also fiscal rules, debt ceilings, and budgetary constitutions.

**Price.** Democratic central bank deficits are a legitimate complaint; Monetary policy mistakes are regular.

**In concept.** The size of the dividend is the subject of the current decision of citizens, and voting for an increase is always locally beneficial. The Alaska dividend illustrates both: it worked according to a statutory formula, and after its politicization it became a field of budgetary struggle.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse.** Here it is especially clear that “the rules can only be changed by the decision of citizens” is not an ornament, but a design defect: the independence of the central bank **is** a refusal to allow the rule to be changed by a current decision.

---

## 19. Insufficient performance

**Now.** The system works at any productivity - it does not imply abundance.

**In concept.** The mechanism requires that the dividend be so large that a significant investment balance remains after consumption. Below the threshold, the design reduces to a conventional mixed economy with additional restrictions.

**Verdict: ⚠️ worse in terms of stability.** Plus a ring dependence: abundance is needed for the operation of the mechanism that was supposed to create this abundance.

---

## 20. War and disaster

**Now.** Directive reallocation: War Production Board of 1942, British Emergency Powers Act, mobilization legislation. The mechanism has been worked out.

**Price.** Authority Ratchet: After a crisis, authority is not fully returned (Higgs).

**In concept.** Emergency mode is necessary, which means there is a built-in transition to a centralized state, and the return path is not described.

**Verdict: ⚠️ worse.** The ratchet problem is general, but your transition leads from the state of “there are no institutions” to the state of “there is one institution with all the powers,” and this is the worst possible start for the reverse movement.

---

## 21. Political passivity

**Now.** The system is designed for passivity: representation, professional apparatus, automatic procedures. A citizen may not participate, and the garbage will still be removed.

**Price.** Alienation from politics, low turnout, a feeling of powerlessness - a legitimate complaint and the motive of your entire structure.

**In concept.** Passivity does not degrade the system smoothly: with a base participation rate of 3–7%, decisions are made by a self-selected group **without a recall mechanism**. An elected politician may not be re-elected, but an activist cannot.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse.** The most unpleasant point: resistance to passivity is not a side effect of representative democracy, but its main function.

---

## 22. Unpleasant but necessary work

**Now.** Compensating pay differences, unions, hazard pay and scheduling, immigration for unpopular positions, mechanization.

**Price.** Works through need: people do hard work because the alternative is worse. This is exactly what you want to get away from, and it is an honest moral challenge to the current system.

**In concept.** Unconditional provision of basic needs closes the channel of need and does not open a new one: status goes to the founders of projects, and not to those who support the existing one.

**Verdict: ⚠️ worse, but the debate is meaningful.** Your counter move: the existing solution is based on the compulsion of need, and it is not a “solution”, but shifting costs onto those who have no choice. The opponent will answer with data on kibbutzim: with equal division and free exit, the most productive ones leave first.

---

## 23. Borders: migration, inheritance, outside world

**Now.** Citizenship, migration law, inheritance tax, exchange rate, foreign trade, treaties.

**In concept.** Not described. Inheritance of savings immediately gives dynastic inequality; the absence of inheritance gives bypass schemes; an inconvertible domestic unit requires a monopoly of foreign trade (another prohibited authority); in a capitalist environment, there is a leakage of the most productive.

**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse (space).** Like point 4, this is not a loss in comparison, but a lack of a section.

## General law, which is visible from the table

See what **all** points with a ⛔ have in common:

| Problem | How does it hold up today | General feature of this institute |
|---|---|---|
| Capital prices | residual contender | suffers irreparable loss |
| Monitoring | concentrated holder or supervision | the benefits of control are concentrated |
| Sewerage and maintenance | tax | does not ask for enthusiasm |
| Science | grant, endowment, tenure | isolated from popularity |
| Long Projects | agreement, charter, concession | ties the hands of the future majority |
| Marketing | short sale, audit | you can make money from the truth |
| Army | ministry, parliament | competing professional hierarchy |
| Inflation | independent Central Bank | withdrawn from current voting |
| Passivity | representation | works without the participation of citizens |

**Each of them is an institution that is deliberately NOT subject to the current wishes of the majority.** This is not an accident or a conspiracy of the elites: this is their function. They exist because problems of this type can only be solved by first tying the hands.

Your concept defines itself by denying exactly this property: “there is no body that independently determines priorities,” “rules are changed only by the decision of citizens,” “funding can be stopped at any time.”

Hence the exact wording of the main objection, and it is stronger than any single mechanism from file 01:

> **The concept systematically removes that class of institutions whose sole function is to limit the current will of the majority. All nine “strictly worse” are the same failure, repeated in nine areas. Therefore, they cannot be repaired separately: any repair is the introduction of an institution with a mandate protected from revision, that is, a rejection of the central principle.**

## Where the concept is really better - and it needs to be defended, not apologised

Three points where the comparison is in your favor. They should be brought forward so that the discussion does not turn into a defense of capitalism:

**✅ 1. Stock inequality and rentier income.** Today, the top 1% owns about a third of net wealth in the United States, the top 10% owns about 70%; property income reproduces itself without the owner's participation, and inheritance makes it dynastic. Your scheme eliminates this channel directly, and not through a tax, which is bypassed everywhere. This is a real advantage, and it is not small.

**✅ 2. Separating survival from employment.** Today, access to basic goods is mediated by the sale of labor, and this creates a constraint that no market advocate defends as a value - only as an inevitability. Unconditional provision removes this. Objections concern feasibility, not desirability.

**✅ 3. Access to capital for new ideas.** Today, venture capital funding is highly concentrated geographically and socially; access is determined by dating networks, collateral, and origins. The ability of any person to submit a project for general consideration is a real increase in access. The question you will be asked is whether this improves the **quality of selection** or just its breadth. The answer is most likely “only breadth” - but breadth is also valuable.

Note that all three benefits relate to **distribution**, and none relate to **production and coordination**. This fits exactly with the general diagnosis: you have a strong theory of distribution and no theory of production.
