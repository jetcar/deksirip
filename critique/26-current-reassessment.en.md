# Current Reassessment of the Model After the Author's Replies

## How objections are selected

An argument is specific to the new system only if:

1. capitalism has a reasonably functioning mechanism for solving the problem;
2. the proposed model actually removes that mechanism;
3. a new causal failure mechanism appears, rather than a general weakness of any complex economy being preserved.

Errors, crises, scarcity, inequality, bureaucracy, external dependence, expert mistakes, and unpleasant work are therefore not objections by themselves.

## What is no longer a blocker

- voluntary maintenance as such;
- ending funding for an unsuccessful project;
- funding science without billionaires;
- managing the army as a public project;
- foreign trade and import shortages;
- closing unnecessary factories;
- the majority not participating in every decision;
- bonuses for dangerous or unpopular work.

These cases still require procedures, but there is no basis for claiming that the model is necessarily worse than capitalism.

## What has already been accepted

- long-term requests and resource competition are resolved through public requests, alternative calculations, and citizens' votes;
- production chains use existing contracts, standards, schedules, logistics, and dispatching;
- the public makes irreversible decisions and bears their consequences;
- unsuccessful or unnecessary projects can be stopped and support can be withdrawn;
- construction starts only with sufficient support from future users or other citizens;
- housing, factories, and land can be reviewed when a more useful or more needed use appears.

These points still need specification, but they are no longer separate conceptual objections.

## Remaining specific issues

### 1. Enforcing citizens' decisions and controlling the apparatus

Rotation, parallel audits, and external funding reduce the risk of organizational autonomy, but they do not answer the extreme case: management refuses an audit or refuses to release a resource after a public decision. An enforcement mechanism with a limited mandate, replaceability, and transparent accountability is still needed.

Transparency does not exclude police, intelligence, auditors, registries, or foreign-trade services. Their grounds, decision logs, and powers must be auditable, while operational data need not be public. The main danger is an apparatus becoming an autonomous center that begins to set goals instead of citizens.

### 2. Automatic review of resource use

Every six months, the system publishes an analysis of the use of land, housing, factories, and infrastructure. Any citizen may initiate a vote to transfer a resource to another user. Details remain open: the term of use, the definition of unused capacity, the voting threshold, and the execution procedure.

### 3. Housing in a particular location and condition

An overall surplus of buildings does not guarantee a suitable apartment in the needed city, size, condition, or proximity to infrastructure. The model still needs rules for comparing such options and for paying relocation when the available housing is elsewhere.

### 4. Legal status of a personal building

Land remains common, while a building may be personal. Exchange is allowed when housing or production conditions are met; letting is allowed only to recover costs. It remains to decide whether this is limited ownership of the building or a long-term use right combined with personal ownership of improvements.

### 5. Changeability of basic rules

The model does not assume a complete pre-written list of eternal rights: rules may be refined as problems appear. This is a conscious political risk — a majority may change previous guarantees. The remaining question is the procedure for doing so: ordinary voting, a higher threshold, repeated voting, or no special procedure.

## Conclusion

The replies do not support the claim that the model is inherently worse than capitalism. Most earlier blockers are addressed by public support, existing production coordination, public responsibility for risk, and automatic review of resources.

Four practical issues remain: enforcement of citizens' decisions, the automatic review procedure, spatial allocation of housing, and the legal status of personal buildings. The changeability of basic rules is a separate, explicit political risk rather than a hidden promise of absolute protection.
