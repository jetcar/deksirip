# Part 9. How strong is selection in real capitalism - a markdown of the argument U13

The file was written after the objection: “having billions, I invest in hundreds of projects, make money on several dozen; many projects are now living without earning anything and having no theoretical opportunity to earn money.”

The objection is correct. My formulation “the loser has less capital in the next round” described an idealized market, not an observed one. Below is what exactly is discounted, what survives, and why you end up with a **different** argument, narrower and more useful.

---

# I. What is true in the objection

**1. Selection does not work on a transactional basis, because venture is a power-law business.**
An investor can be wrong 90% of the time and still be extremely successful: according to Horsley Bridge, about 6% of transactions generate about 60% of the industry's profitability. This means that “made a mistake → lost influence” at the level of an individual decision is not carried out at all. Feedback is only available at the portfolio level, and with a lag of 7–12 years.

**2. Wealth is a buffer against selection, and the greater the capital, the thicker it is.**
Selection pressure on an allocator is inversely proportional to its size. The owner of billions can survive a decade of continuous mistakes. The owner of ten thousand will not survive one. This means that selection in capitalism acts most strongly on those who have the least influence over distribution, and weakest on those who have the most. Exactly the opposite of what is required to train the system.

**3. Unprofitable companies survive for years and decades - this is the norm, not an anomaly.**
The share of “zombies” among public companies in developed countries, according to the Bank of International Settlements, grew to approximately 15% by the end of the 2010s. Uber achieved annual operating profit approximately 14 years after its founding. Amazon operated with near-zero profits for two decades by design. A significant part of the biotech and almost the entire crypto market consists of companies that have no path to profit, even theoretically.

**4. Inheritance breaks selection completely.**
The heir of a bad allocator receives the entire capital. At each generation, the connection between the quality of decisions and the amount of resources available is broken. This, by the way, is a point **in favor of your concept**, and not against: your scheme eliminates dynastic stewards, and this is a real improvement in selection through one specific channel.

**5. Selection is often based not on the quality of solutions, but on access.**
The persistence of returns at venture capital firms is concentrated in a handful of names, and is due in large part to better deal flow rather than better selection skills: a strong fund attracts strong founders because it is strong. This is the Matthew effect—accumulation of advantage rather than selection for accuracy.

Add to this the bailouts (2008, 2020), monopoly rents and regulatory capture, which sever the connection between a firm's survival and the quality of its decisions.

---

#II. What U13s don't survive?

Direct markdown, without mitigation:

**The statement “capitalism disciplines the allocation of capital through residual risk” does not survive.** As an empirical description of the last fifteen years, this is incorrect. Discipline was weak and sometimes non-existent.

**The conclusion “no selection ⇒ the system falls apart” does not survive.** We have a full-scale experiment: 2010–2021, near-zero rates, abundance of capital, extremely weak discipline. The economy did not fall into deficit according to Kornai. It made notable misplacements—zombies, WeWork, SPACs, crypto—but continued to operate and produce real results, including major technology shifts.

**Consequently, U13 can no longer be formulated as an impossibility.** The correct formulation is quantitative: the absence of selection results in the accumulation of incorrect placement at a certain rate, and the debate is about the speed, and not about the very fact of performance. This is significantly weaker than I stated in the last message.

Accordingly, two verdicts in file 07 are softened: point 1 (prices of capital goods) and point 3 (stringency of the budget constraint) overestimated the quality of the existing solution. See section VI.

---

#III. What Survives - Three Narrow Statements

**1. The difference between “weak” and “zero” remains, and it is not cosmetic.**
The selection coefficient in capitalism is low, but strictly positive, and most importantly, **recoverable**. It rose sharply in 2022-2024, when rising capital costs killed off an entire cohort of unprofitable companies. The recovery mechanism exists and has been applied in our memory.

In your system, the coefficient is structurally equal to zero, and there is not a single recovery mechanism: there is no rate, there is no creditor, there is no moment when someone will demand the money back. The era of cheap money was a **temporary suspension** of selection with the switch preserved. Your design is a permanent suspension without a switch.

**2. Capitalism has a gender that you don't: there is always someone who basically wants their money back.**
A zombie company lives not because there is no claim, but because the creditor tolerates it. Tolerance is the solution, and it resonates: that's exactly what happened in 2022. There is not a single participant in your system whose tolerance could be revoked, because no one expected a return in the first place. The difference is not how strict the mechanism is, but whether it exists in an off state or does not exist at all.

**3. A professional skeptic only exists where there is a return claim.**
Short sellers, credit analysts, activists all make money from discovering overvaluations. In a decade of cheap money, shorting was unprofitable, short-strategy funds declined radically - and fraud increased: FTX, a wave of SPACs with unreliable forecasts. This is not a coincidence, but a direct connection: if they removed earnings from exposure, they got an increase in undetected deception.

Your system makes this loss **permanent and constructive** rather than cyclical. Of the three surviving points, this is, in my opinion, the strongest, because it is confirmed by natural experiment, and not derived from theory.

---

#IV. Correct analogy: not venture, but the grant sector

Your remark that many projects live without even a theoretical opportunity to earn money is not a defect in your system. This is her **plan**: to finance what is necessary, not what is profitable. Basic science, infrastructure, art, rare diseases - all this in today's economy is financed outside the repayment mechanism: grants, endowments, philanthropy, and the budget.

Hence a more honest and more useful formulation of the question.

> **Your concept is not a “venture without owners.” This is a proposal to run the entire economy as a grant sector.**

And then the dispute ceases to be theoretical, because the grant sector exists, it is large, and its quality has been measured. What we know about him:

- **It produces real results.** All basic science, a significant part of medicine, genome mapping, accelerators, space. It is empirically impossible to say that distribution without return does not work.
- **He systematically misses the mark on priorities.** Disease research funding correlates with visibility and empathy, not disease burden. This is a stable, repeatedly measured distortion.
- **It is subject to fashions more than the market sector** because there is no price that would indicate that a fashion has ended.
- **Its quality rests on the peer review procedure**, that is, on a professional institute, and not on the decision of the general public. Take away peer review, and the grant sector turns into philanthropy by sympathy, the quality of which is noticeably lower.

**Realistic prediction for your system:** philanthropic sector-level resource allocation quality applied to the entire economy. Not a disaster or paralysis - functional, but with a steady bias towards the noticeable and charming, with fashions and without a mechanism to announce the end of fashion.

This is a much more accurate prediction than “the system will fall apart”, and, by the way, it is more testable in discussion.

---

# V. Why the era of cheap money is the main argument, not a counterargument

It's worth recording, because in an argument it will turn against you.

2010-2021 is the closest approximation to your system that humanity has put: capital in abundance, return requirement almost disabled, placement follows the narrative. What was observed:

- capital rushed to attention and history (crypto, meme stocks, SPAC, WeWork);
- fraud has increased because it has become unprofitable to expose;
- the share of companies without a path to profit has increased;
- Aggregate productivity grew slowly, despite the abundance of capital.

Your opponent will say: here is your system, look at the result. Your honest answer should be: this period does not show a collapse, but a **type of distortion** - a shift towards narrative. And this type of distortion is exactly what you should expect from your design, just all the time, not ten out of forty years.

#VII. What does U13 sound like now?

Old wording (cancelled):
> The ban on accumulation is a ban on training the system; the market corrects errors by selecting allocators.

New wording:
> **The selection of allocators in capitalism is weak, noisy, dampened by the size of capital and terminated by inheritance - but exists in a switchable state and can be restored. In the proposed system, it is structurally equal to zero and irrecoverable: there is no rate, there is no creditor, there is no moment of claim, and therefore there is no professional skeptic, since he exists only where there is a claim for return. The expected result is not paralysis, but a constant deployment of resources with the quality of the philanthropic sector: workable, with a steady shift towards visibility, with mods and without a signal about their end.**

This is weaker than I argued, and it is still an objection that is not eliminated by setting the parameters: the selection switch can only be restored by entering a return claim, that is, capital.
