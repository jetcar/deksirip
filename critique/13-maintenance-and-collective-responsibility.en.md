# Part 13. Maintenance, "necessary" and general responsibility

Objection: even now no one goes to the right place, business doesn’t make money there, government programs are done at a minimum, and often not done until problems arise. This means there is no working mechanism now - there is interest, and then it works, or it doesn’t, and then it works until it breaks. Now everything lies with the officials, who may or may not do it. With shared responsibility, “not to do” quickly becomes a problem – this is evident in apartment associations. The only danger is that someone can do everything themselves and well, and with his departure everyone will suddenly understand that it did not work on its own.

# I. Scored: I overestimated the existing solution

In file 07, I gave point 7 a verdict ⛔ and wrote that the tax works because it does not require enthusiasm. This is only half true, and you have pointed out the other half exactly.

The existing system serves the infrastructure poorly. The political cycle systematically prefers putting a ribbon on a new bridge over repairing an old one: the new is visible and attributed to the incumbent, the repair is invisible and benefits the successor. The accumulated underrepairs in developed countries are measured in trillions. “Don’t do it until problems arise” is an accurate description.

And your stronger move is also correct: **interest is a necessary condition in both systems.** The difference is not that in some places the mechanism works automatically and in others it does not.

But she is there, and she is in a different place.

---

#II. Where exactly is the floor of the existing system?

“An official may or may not do something” is an inaccurate description. He can do bad, late and little. He cannot do nothing at all, and three things hold him back, none of which is enthusiasm:

**1. Duty of Service.** The water utility has a legal duty to supply water. Failure to file is a violation, followed by a regulatory order, a lawsuit, or a fine. The obligation does not depend on anyone's interest and is not canceled by lack of desire.

**2. Automatic revenue.** The tariff is collected from consumers regardless of whether anyone remembers the existence of the pumping station. Money comes without making a decision. This is key: maintenance is not funded by attention, but by the flow that flows by default.

**3. Personal responsibility for refusal.** If the bridge collapses, a specific person will be responsible: they will find him, name him, remove him, attract him. It works weakly, but it works - and most importantly, the next one in this position saw how the previous one ended up.

**Which of these is in the concept? Nothing.** There is no legal obligation because there is no obligated person. There is no automatic proceeds because funding is voluntary by design. There is no personal responsibility, because the decision was made by an atomized mass (file 08, section IV).

**The exact wording of the dispute:** not “a working mechanism versus a non-working one”, but **a “low-sex mechanism versus a non-sex mechanism”**. “They do the minimum” - this is the description of gender. You describe it and call it absence.

#III. Fellowships: Your example is the best possible and it shows three things

The example is well chosen: it is the closest observable analogue of shared service responsibility. Therefore, it is worth disassembling it completely and not brushing it aside.

## 1. It works on a mandatory contribution, not on a voluntary one

The basis of the partnership is **mandatory** payment. The maintenance fee cannot be waived. That's why the roof is being repaired.

And the characteristic failure of the system is precisely non-payments: chronic debt, and the legal response to it is court, collection, restriction of services. That is, the working part of the partnership is forced, not voluntary.

In your concept, the contribution is voluntary. This is not “the same as a partnership,” it is a partnership from which what it rests on has been taken away.

## 2. Its characteristic catastrophic failure is deferred maintenance

The Champlain Towers South condominium in Surfside, Florida, collapsed in June 2021, killing 98 people. An engineering survey in 2018 revealed serious damage to load-bearing structures. The cost of repairs grew, the owners resisted the special contribution for years because no one wanted to pay; the board changed; approval was delayed until the collapse.

This is not a rare case, but a pure demonstration of the mechanism: **shared responsibility → consumption is visible and painful now, failure is painless now → postponement → disaster**.

The reaction is also indicative. Florida in 2022 legislatively **abolished the ability of owners to vote to refuse the formation of a reserve** for structural elements and introduced mandatory inspections according to the timing. Direction of correction: from voluntary collective decision to coercion. The legislator looked at the voluntary version and **removed the vote**.

## 3. Your own disclaimer is not “out of danger”, but the main mode of operation

You wrote: the danger is that someone can do everything themselves and well, but with his departure everyone will suddenly understand that it did not work on its own.

This is not a collateral risk. This is a **typical mode of existence of a partnership**: one dedicated person pulls everything, the rest do not participate, and when he leaves, the structure crumbles. This means that the observed mechanism is not “shared responsibility works”, but **“the personal devotion of one person replaces the institution”**.

You cannot build a system on this for three reasons:
- the enthusiast is not reproduced by the procedure, its appearance is random;
- it cannot be assigned, verified or replaced;
- his work is not paid or recognized, so burnout is the norm, not the exception.

You called it danger. I would say it more harshly: this is the only thing that actually works there, and it is not extensible.

#IV. Why can’t we extrapolate from a partnership to society?

Partnership is the **best** possible case for your concept. See what makes it favorable:

| Condition | Partnership | City sewerage / resistance monitoring / transformer replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Group Boundaries | clear, 50–200 people | blurred, millions |
| Is the defect visible in person | yes, my ceiling is leaking | no, I never see the substation |
| Feedback delay | hours–days | years–decades |
| Personal experience of consequences | direct | indirect, often from other people |
| Social pressure | face to face, neighbors | missing |
| Who's watching | all living, constantly | nobody |

These are exactly Elinor Ostrom’s principles for creating successful shared resources: clear resource and group boundaries, observability, monitoring, proportionate sanctions. The partnership satisfies them - and therefore sometimes works. The national infrastructure does not satisfy anyone.

**Extrapolation goes in the opposite direction:** every parameter that makes a partnership workable degrades when moving to the scale of society. Your example does not prove “this is scalable”, but “this works exactly under conditions that do not exist on a societal scale.”

#VI. Clarification of the verdict: point 7 should be divided in two

My previous ⛔ was too rude. Correct division:

**7a. Local visible infrastructure** - yard, road in the area, water connection, playground, local repairs.
**Verdict: ✅ The concept is probably better.** Empirics are on your side: the participatory budget in Porto Alegre brought real improvements in water supply and sanitation in poor areas, because residents knew exactly where the problem was, but officials did not. Here, citizens are more informed than the apparatus, and transferring decisions to them improves the result.

**7b. Invisible long-latency systems** - building design reserves, transformer replacement, antibiotic resistance surveillance, critical node redundancy, protection against rare events.
**Verdict: ⛔ strictly worse, and remains.** The current system has a low but non-zero floor of duty, automatic tariff and personal responsibility. The concept of gender has no gender at all. Surfside shows what happens with shared responsibility without coercion - and shows a group of two hundred people who see the cracks firsthand.

The score in file 07 changes to **6 strictly worse, 9 worse, 5 parity, 4 better** (item 7 is divided, 7a becomes an asset).
