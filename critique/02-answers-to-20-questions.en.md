# Part 2. Answers to 20 asked questions

Links like M7 are to mechanisms from [01-critique-core.md](01-critique-core.md).

---

**1. What mechanisms will lead to concentration of power?**
Four independent channels, each sufficient on its own:
(a) delegation of project evaluation → curators (M4);
(b) control without ownership within financed projects (M3);
(c) agenda-setting: who formulates the question of reserves and rules (M11);
(d) registries - identities, citizenship, balances, as well as the infrastructure of AI advisors (M19, M14).
Plus the army as the only coherent hierarchy (M15).

**2. Can individual accumulation turn back into economic inequality?**
Yes, in three ways: convertibility leaks (M9); inheritance or bypass (M9); *flow* inequality when differentiating pay for unpopular work (M17). But the main thing is that the inequality of the supply is not even necessary: ​​the inequality of the *directed flow*, that is, the number of other people’s units that follow your recommendation (M4), is enough.

**3. Can economic inequality turn into political inequality?**
It is already political by design. In your system, resource allocation **is** politics: assign units = make a political decision. This means that anyone who influences the direction of someone else’s units is a political subject. There is no “economics/politics” division that could be protected by regulation at all.

**4. What happens to projects that are useful but unpopular?**
They are not funded. This is a structural feature, not a glitch. Sewage, vector control, tuberculosis surveillance, bridge maintenance, waste disposal, legacy infrastructure maintenance - low visibility, diffuse benefit, zero narrative. Empirical reference point: philanthropy. Disease research funding correlates with empathy and visibility rather than disease burden (breast cancer vs. COPD and liver disease; ALS ice-bucket vs. sepsis).

**5. How to finance basic science?**
The proposed mechanism - not at all, on an observable scale. Science crowdfunding tested: Experiment.com and its peers - median project raises thousands of dollars, thematic bias towards fascinating objects (dinosaurs, large mammals) versus boring and important ones (parasitology, wastewater surveillance). Basic science requires (a) expert judgment inaccessible to the layman, (b) tolerance for zero results, (c) portfolio thinking, (d) a horizon longer than the attention cycle. All four properties are directly opposite to the properties of the mechanism “citizens direct the dividend to what interests them.”

**6. How to finance projects with returns over decades?**
See M10. An irrevocable commitment is required, and an irrevocable multi-year pool is a non-controlled fund, that is, a prohibited entity. A formal dilemma without an intermediate solution.

**7. How to create strategic reserves?**
A permanent apparatus is needed: product specification, contracts, warehouses, rotation of expired items, tests, decision on release. This is an agency with discretion. A stronger argument: Even states that **had** a special body with a legal mandate and budget did not create adequate supplies for 2020, even though pandemic plans existed everywhere. If a mechanism with concentrated responsibility fails, a mechanism that distributes responsibility to everyone and therefore to no one will fail more (M11, M12).

**8. How to prevent manipulation through propaganda and marketing?**
No way - and this is worse than in capitalism. In capitalism, marketing competes for consumer demand, and capital is distributed by professionals who suffer losses. Your marketing fights **directly for capital**. The return per ruble of persuasion becomes maximum in the economy. Plus, there is no professional skeptic, because you can’t make money by exposing an overrated project (M5, Grossman-Stiglitz). Prediction: the share of the social product spent on persuasion grows until the marginal return equals the production one.

**9. Could AI itself become the new center of power?**
Yes, and for two different reasons. (a) Agenda power: whoever determines which options are shown to the citizen at all determines the outcome (M11). (b) Destruction of error independence: the general advisor correlates judgments and kills the epistemic advantage of the mass decision (M14). The statement “AI is not an arbiter of truth” is a declaration, not an institutional mechanism. The question that needs to be answered is: *who trains the model, who can change it, and by what procedure - and why this procedure itself is not a prohibited organ.*

**10. How to prevent the army from seizing power?**
Known remedies that work: a strong civilian apparatus with budgetary authority, competing power structures, party control, rotation of command, a conscript army rooted in society. Your design cancels or weakens almost every (M15). “The power to act without voting in an attack” gives the command the right to determine whether an attack has occurred—this is Schmitt’s definition of sovereignty.

**11. How to prevent organized minorities from seizing resources?**
No way: Olson (M12). Moreover, your mechanism gives the organized minority a **more direct** tool than in a representative system: there is no need to buy a politician, you just need to coordinate your own dividends. No mechanism known to me eliminates the asymmetry of concentrated benefits and dispersed costs; it can only be compensated by institutions that you do not have.

**12. How to avoid the emergence of a new bureaucracy?**
It won’t work, and this follows from your own requirements. Transparency of the chain “decision → rule → data → calculation → distribution” requires: maintaining registers, auditing, data standards, dispute resolution, antifraud, personal identification, warehouse rotation, enforcement of property bans. This is Weber's definition of bureaucracy. The only difference is that you refuse to give it formal status, and therefore formal accountability.

**13. How to take into account real physical resources, and not conventional units?**
Dispute between Neurath and Mises about calculation in kind. The problem is not one of computational power (an objection removed by Cockshott-Cottrell), but that technical coefficients are not given in advance: they are endogenous to the choice of technology, quality is multidimensional and not reducible to a scalar, and knowledge of conditions is diffuse and partially inarticulate (Hayek 1945). Historical scale: The State Planning Committee compiled about several thousand balance sheets for millions of commodity items. In addition, your system wants **and** monetary units, **and** physical accounting: if a unit does not clear the physical market, there is a shortage; if it clears it, you need the one who bears the loss, that is, the owner (M1).

**14. How to avoid inflation when distributing dividends?**
See M8. The key problem is not the issue technique, but the fact that the size of the dividend becomes the subject of voting, and voting for its increase is always locally beneficial. What is needed is a statutory formula removed from the current vote—that is, a constitutional limitation that the concept explicitly rejects (M18).

**15. What if performance isn't good enough?**
Then the dividend is small, savings are small, the investment mechanism does not work, and the usual mixed economy remains with additional restrictions. Worse: the system has a startup problem. High automation requires huge capital investments; the investment mechanism is the weakest point of the design (M1, M2, M5). That is, a prerequisite for abundance is needed for the mechanism that was supposed to create this abundance to work.

**16. What happens during a war or disaster?**
Centralization with ratchet (M16). The existence of a mandatory emergency mode is a built-in path of conversion to a centralized state, and there is no return path described in the design.

**17. What if part of the population becomes politically passive?**
Not "part", but the majority - this is the base rate, not the risk (M13). The result is a self-selected elite without a recall mechanism, that is, in terms of accountability it is worse than a representative democracy.

**18. What incentives will people have to do unpleasant work?**
See M17. Plus a separate effect: the system directly increases the status of “project” activities and resets the status of routine maintenance. Infrastructure breaks down not where new things are not built, but where old ones are not maintained.

**19. Different ideas about usefulness?**
For private consumption this is not a problem - everyone spends their own. The problem begins exactly where public goods are needed: defense, reserves, science, infrastructure. There, aggregation is required, and Arrow and McKelvey (M11) are included. Plus Sen's paradox: minimal individual rights are incompatible with the Pareto principle with an unlimited range of preferences. These results are not by design.

**20. Are there theories that actually describe this system?**
Yes, and quite accurately. More details in [04-prior-art-and-counterexamples.md](04-prior-art-and-counterexamples.md). In short: it's **John Roemer's coupon socialism** (1994) plus **universal basic income** plus **participatory budget** for public goods. Also close: Lange-Lerner's market socialism, David Schweickart's "Economic Democracy", James Juncker's pragmatic market socialism, James Meade's "Agatotopia", Oscar Lange's social dividend, Eric Olin Wright's "Real Utopias", plural/quadratic funding RadicalxChange.

The important takeaway from this: **you do not have an original concept, but a well-known family of models with a well-known list of objections.** The most useful thing to do before publication is to read Roemer and understand why he had to add monitor banks, and Schweickart - regional investment funds with a professional apparatus. Both authors started with your intuition and both were forced to introduce exactly the institution that you prohibit.
