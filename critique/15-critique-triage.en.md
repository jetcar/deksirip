# Triage of criticism: what is removed, what is not a blocker, what is a blocker

> **Updated after author's replies (08/27/2026).** The following old triage is preserved as argumentation history, but its section C is no longer the current blocker list. After the introduction of worker self-government, resource claims, public audit and the recognition of ordinary crises as common to any economy, only construction-specific nodes remain on the current list. The current edition is given below.

## Current status after author's replies

### Filmed as an argument against the model

- **Voluntary service and unpleasant labor.** This is not automatically worse than the current system; bonuses, operating expenses, management changes and public liability are compatible with the model.
- **“No one controls the plant.”** Clarification accepted: the operating enterprise is managed by the organizer/management chosen by the employees; External specialists are invited as needed.
- **Revocability of science funding.** For scientific projects, regular milestone budgets are acceptable; billionaire and irrevocable commitment are not necessary.
- **Army, foreign trade, deficits and crises.** The very presence of these problems is not specific: capitalism also does not eliminate them. In the new model, they require a transparent overall budget and predefined procedures.
- **Hidden capital through savings.** The objection remains only when it is possible to convert the balance into ownership, employment, political influence or inherited power. When such rights are prohibited, this is not a blocker, but a matter of transaction rules.

###Specific design issues remain

1. **Execution of citizens' decisions.** Rotation, parallel audit and external financing reduce the risk of autonomy, but an enforcement mechanism is needed in case the organization refuses to audit or releases a resource.
2. **Automatic review of resources.** Once every six months, the system publishes an analysis of the use of land, housing, factories and infrastructure; any citizen can initiate a vote on the transfer of a resource.
3. **Housing in a specific location and quality.** The total number of houses may be sufficient, but the desired apartment may not be in the desired city, size or condition; matching and moving rules need to be defined.
4. **Legal status of a personal building.** The land is common, the building can be personal, exchange is limited by conditions, delivery is allowed only at cost; All that remains is to choose the legal form.
5. **Variability of basic rules.** The complete list of eternal rights is not fixed in advance; All that remains is to accept the risk of the majority changing the guarantees and determine the procedure for such a change.

### Historically proposed mechanism for competition for steel

This section has been saved as a fixed stage of the discussion. The author considers the very principle of public choice between competing applications to have already been decided; Only the technical details of execution and revision are open.

Each project publishes not only the amount, but also a calendar of needs by resource type. Manufacturers publish capacity forecasts, inventories and confirmed deliveries. The system shows the available volume and opportunity cost of each order. When there is a shortage, citizens choose between specific projects, seeing what exactly is being postponed. A confirmed application gives a conditional reservation, not ownership; reservations are reviewed periodically. For maintenance and basic consumption, democratically approved priorities and power reserves can be established in advance.

This does not eliminate scarcity, but it does turn it into an explicit, testable choice between alternatives. The remaining question is how exactly to calculate opportunity cost and who approves the priorities; This is a design problem, not a ready-made counterexample.

A summary of thirteen parsing files, sorted by practical significance. Compiled taking into account the clarified purpose of the system (maintenance and development, and not the accumulation of capital) - this amendment removed some of the objections and did not affect others.

# A. Removed or significantly discounted

These objections were raised and **failed**. You shouldn’t refer to them in discussions, but if your opponent does, here’s how to respond.

**A1. “The market disciplines the allocation of capital by selecting investors.”**
Discounted (file 09). Selection by deal does not work: about 6% of transactions provide about 60% of the industry’s profitability, that is, you can be wrong in nine cases out of ten and be successful. Wealth dampens selection pressure the more strongly the greater it is. The share of zombies among public companies in developed countries reached approximately 15%. Inheritance breaks the connection between the quality of decisions and the amount of resources at each generation.
The main thing: 2010–2021 was a period of almost disabled discipline, and the economy functioned. The conclusion “without selection the system collapses” has been refuted.

**A2. “Transparency is no substitute for market signals.”**
Shot for regular reproducible products (file 10). There the signal is cheap, unambiguous, fast and attributable; unsold warehouse is visible to everyone without analysis. The selection of allocators in this segment is redundant.

**A3. “The coalition of employed people will not allow the failed project to be stopped.”**
Filmed in tranches (file 11). With tranche financing, the silence is reversed: the money does not flow if no one has taken the action. Continuation requires activity, stopping happens on its own.

**A4. “There will be inflation from the distribution of dividends.”**
Filmed at standardized prices (file 08). There will literally be no inflation. The problem remains, but in a different form - see B4.

**A5. “We need a permanent body for strategic reserves.”**
Significantly weakened (file 07, item 11). The body **was**: with a statutory mandate and a protected budget. The supply of respirators used up in 2009 has not been replenished by 2020. Pandemic plans existed almost everywhere, adequate supplies almost nowhere. The argument “give it to the people with a mandate” is countered by the fact that people with a mandate have already failed the task.

**A6. Calculation problem in relation to maintenance.**
Removed by clarifying the target (this file). Maintenance requirements are technically definable: transformer service life, replacement regulations, strength standards. We don't need prices here, we need engineering standards and data. Mises' computational problem remains acute for **development** and is almost irrelevant for **maintenance**.

**A7. Inheritance.**
Point in favor of the concept (file 09). Dynastic inheritance in the existing system completely breaks the connection between the quality of decisions and the volume of managed resources. The concept eliminates this.

# B. Not blockers

Real problems, but: either they exist to the same extent now, or it is a question of degree and price, not performance.

**B1. Economic inequality turns into political inequality.** Parity. Now: lobbying is about $4 billion a year, the election cycle is about $15 billion, the independent influence of the median citizen on politics is statistically close to zero (Gilens and Page). You remove one channel and create another; It is impossible to say that the second one is worse without a model.

**B2. AI will become the new center of power.** Parity in fact. Recommendation algorithms are already setting the agenda for billions. Your specific version of the objection is weaker and narrower: the system makes AI a **mandatory** element of the decision loop, whereas it is now optional.

**B3. Externalities and physical resources.** Parity. It’s not decided now (climate is the biggest example), you haven’t decided. As an argument against, it doesn’t work; as your advantage too.

**B4. Deficits and money overhang instead of inflation.** Real problem, but not a blocker: deficit economies have existed for decades. Mechanism: with fixed prices, excess demand manifests itself as a queue, and not as a rise in prices; the accumulated balances form a canopy, which is realized with any change of rules. Separately: **the quick public signal that the dividend is too high** disappears. This impairs controllability, but does not make the system inoperable.

**B5. Soft budget constraint.** A matter of degree. Now soft restrictions are also more widespread than is commonly thought (about 15% of public companies are zombies, bailouts in 2008 and 2020). The gap is smaller than it seemed.

**B6. Organized minorities will take over distribution.** Worse than now, but still bad: industry lobbies, regulatory capture, agricultural subsidies. Your capture costs are lower and your resistance is weaker. Hard, but not a blocker.

**B7. New bureaucracy.** Worse, but not fatal. The apparatus is necessary (registries, audit, anti-fraud, identification), and it will not be recognized by the body, which means it will not be formally accountable. This is a deterioration compared to formalized bureaucracy, but the system lives with it.

**B8. Market failures like antibiotics** Rather in your favor. Here the market has openly failed, and treatment is no longer market-based - bonuses, guaranteed purchases, orphan legislation. Your mechanism addresses this class of tasks directly.

**B9. Local visible infrastructure.** In your favor. The participatory budget in Porto Alegre produced real improvements in water supply and sanitation in poor areas precisely because residents knew where the problem was, but the apparatus did not.

# C. Blockers

Objections that are not eliminated by setting parameters and are not removed by clarifying the goal. Arranged according to strength.

##C1. Maintenance rate: 15–16% versus 2%

**The strongest point, and clarifying the purpose does not remove it.**

Your amendment “what is needed is not accumulation, but maintenance” reduces what is required, but not by an order of magnitude. Consumption of fixed capital - that is, depreciation, that is, exactly **the cost of maintaining the system in an unchanged state** - amounts to about 15-16% of GDP in developed economies. Gross capital formation in the United States is about 21%, of which the net increase is about 5%.

That is: giving up growth reduces the requirement from ~21% to ~15–16%. Zero growth ≠ low investment; this is still a sixth of the total social product, directed to ensuring that nothing degrades.

The observed rate of voluntary giving of funds without expectation of return - charity in the United States, the most generous of rich countries - is about 2% of GDP.

**Mechanism.** This is free riding, not greed: the capital stock is a public good, my personal contribution is negligible, it will not suffer noticeably from my refusal. Plus a third option, dominant for the inattentive, that is, for the majority: just saving up - it doesn’t require effort, analysis of reports and the risk of embarrassment from failure.

**Why it can’t be adjusted.** There are exactly two known ways to maintain a high rate: coercion (mandatory deduction) or reward (interest, profit). Section 11 of the concept prohibits the first, section 1 the second.

**What can be answered:** only empirically. We need cases where the voluntary allocation of funds without return consistently provided at least 8–10% of GDP. I don’t know anything like this, and this is the most useful request for sites.

##C2. Delegation leads to concentration, and tranches reinforce this

There are thousands of projects, a citizen’s time is finite. Delegation is inevitable; the returns to specialization in attention are cumulative; the distribution of delegated shares becomes power-law.

Arithmetic: a project worth 10 billion with a horizon of 12 years with quarterly tranches - about 48 decisions. With 20 projects per person, this means 80 reviews of technical reports per year, each in their own specialty. This is a part-time job.

**Tranching does not solve the problem, but increases the load that creates it.**

Empirics: LiquidFeedback superdelegates in the German Pirate Party; concentration of delegates in DAO with participation of 1–10%; The Big Three funds with about 20% of S&P 500 voting power.

Prohibit delegation = demand the impossible. Allow = gain concentration. Restrict = bypass through lists of recommendations, prohibiting which means prohibiting the publication of opinions.

##C3. Invisible infrastructure with long latency: no floor

Shared with local infrastructure (which is in your favor, B9).

Now the low but **non-zero** floor is held by three things, none of which are enthusiastic: duty of service (legal, with an order and a claim for violation); automatic tariff (money comes without making a decision); personal responsibility for refusal. The concept does not include any of the three.

**The key counterexample is your own.** Apartment associations operate on a **compulsory** fee; their typical failure is delayed repairs; Champlain Towers South in Surfside collapsed in 2021 after three years of owner resistance to special assessment, 98 dead; Florida responded by legislating **removing the right to vote away the reserve**. And the typical mode of partnership is that one enthusiast carries on with everything, and when he leaves, it falls apart, as you yourself noted.

Partnership is the most favorable case (the boundaries are clear, the defect is visible in person, feedback is on the clock, neighbors are pressing). City sewerage and resistance surveillance do not satisfy any condition.

##C4. Dilemma of silence between tranches

Tranching fixes inertia, but requires choosing a default, and both ends are bad:

- **default “continue”** (the citizen set the deduction for 5 years) → returned to inertia and the continuation coalition;
- **default “stop”** → each project anew wins attention every quarter, not the good one survives, but the **memorable one**; a twenty-year project must wage a twenty-year campaign of attention retention without having anything to show for it.

The second option hits fundamental science the hardest—that is, section 6 of the concept, for the sake of which the long-term financing mechanism was introduced.

Intermediate options are points on the segment between two bad ends, and not a solution.

##C5. Nobody manages the operating enterprise

Section 4 describes the financing of **new** projects. Who in a working factory decides about replacing a machine, hiring a supplier, or changing a product is not described.

Three options, all tested: the labor collective (Yugoslav self-government: the Ward-Domar-Vanek effect, the Furubotn-Pejovic horizon problem, the collapse of the system); designated manager (prohibited body); nobody.

This is a **gap, not a loss in comparison**, and the first thing a competent economist will notice.

##C6. The setter agent is irremovable

Section 7 requires that citizens set priorities for reserves, and at the same time that there is no prioritization authority. With a multidimensional space of alternatives and a majority rule, the core is usually empty (McKelvey's theorem), and the controller of the voting sequence leads the outcome almost anywhere.

Someone formulates a question, determines “sufficient volume,” selects a supplier, maintains a warehouse, rotates out-of-date items, and decides when to release. This is an organ with discretion. Denying its existence does not eliminate it, but deprives it of accountability.

##C7. Civilian control of the army requires a competing hierarchy

Civil control has never been exercised directly by citizens anywhere. It is carried out by **another professional hierarchy**: a ministry with civilian leadership, parliament with budgetary authority, military justice, a procedure for the appointment and dismissal of command.

Section 9 abolishes the professional political class, that is, the second hierarchy, leaving the first. This is the praetorian configuration: the army becomes the only permanent, disciplined organization.

Separately: “acts without voting during an attack” gives the command the right to determine the fact of an attack.

##C8. Empire building instead of theft

You block the assignment of stock - that's right. But Section 12 declares status and recognition to be the main incentive, meaning the reward is the very fact of managing a large project. A rational strategy is not to appropriate, but to **grow**.

This is worse than theft: theft is limited to what can be hidden and underground; the growth of the project is unlimited, public and approved; Theft has a victim who will complain; overgrowth has a victim - unbuilt alternatives that no one sees.

Plus a historical channel of appropriation where one cannot own: consumption through position. Service apartment, dacha, transport, special supplies. And tying a benefit to a position **tightens** the struggle for a position, because if it is lost, everything is lost - the owner can sell the asset and leave, the manager cannot.

# D. General structure of blockers

Please note: **C1, C3, C4, C6, C7 are the same failure** repeated in different areas.

Today, each of these tasks is solved by an institution with a common property: it is **deliberately not subordinate to the current will of the majority**. Mandatory deduction does not require enthusiasm. The treaty ties the hands of the future majority. The Department of Defense is a competing hierarchy. The foundation's charter was withdrawn from voting.

Section 11 of the concept defines itself through the negation of exactly this property.

Hence the practical conclusion: **it is impossible to fix these points individually.** Any repair means the introduction of an institution with a mandate protected from revision. The decision is made once and at the section 11 level, rather than five times at the machinery level.

**A fork in the road that needs to be stated explicitly:**

- either section 11 remains in its current form, and then C1, C3, C4, C6, C7 are accepted as the known price;
- or it is recognized that some of the compulsions are legitimate (for example: mandatory contributions for the maintenance of infrastructure, irrevocable mandates for long-term projects, a constitutional level with an increased threshold for change), and then the five blockers turn into tasks of designing accountability.

The second path leads to a system that can be described as follows: unconditional dividend + cooperative sector + public investment funds with a mandate + participatory budget at the local level. This is an existing, debated and partially tested position. It is stronger than the original formulation - and does not contain what made the original idea attractive.

The choice between these paths is not technical, but value-based, and the author must make it.
