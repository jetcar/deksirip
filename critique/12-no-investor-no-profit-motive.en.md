# Part 12. “There is no investor” - what it eliminates and what it creates

Objection: there is no investor, and this is the main thing. There is no motive to give money until the project is interesting; There is no need to profit from investments, so you don’t have to invest at all. And asking for money in order to write it off is pointless - you can’t build a castle for yourself, unless you immediately declare the project “build me a castle.”

Two statements and they give opposite results. You are blocking the appropriation of the stock - this is true and this is serious. The absence of an obligation to invest creates a new problem, and it is, in my opinion, more severe than all the previous ones.

---

# I. What is true: you cannot accumulate stolen goods

If the units do not buy the means of production, are not inherited and are not converted, then the appropriation of 10 billion as a **stock** is meaningless. The classic “bring out and own” scheme does not work. This is a real design property, not a declaration.

An important thing to follow from this is that the type of fraud that I cited in File 09 as an argument (FTX, the SPAC wave) is really difficult in your system. Liquid reserves were appropriated there. There is no stock here.

But we have a natural experiment for seventy years and hundreds of millions of people: a system where private ownership of the means of production was prohibited, but someone still had to manage the resources. The question “what is appropriated where one cannot own” has a documented answer, and it is not “nothing.”

---

#II. Assigned by flow, not by stock

**Consumption through a position.** Resources are not withdrawn - they are consumed on behalf of the project. A company car, a company apartment, a company dacha, business trips, hospitality expenses, departmental medicine, special supplies, equipment that ends up at home. Nobody owns anything; The standard of living differs exponentially.

This is not an anomaly of the Soviet system, but a structural feature of it: **when one cannot own, privilege takes the form of access.** And note the detail that turns your argument around: since benefits are tied to position, not property, when one loses one's position, they disappear entirely. This makes the holder **more** desperate to hold the position, not less. The owner can sell the asset and leave. The manager cannot - he loses everything, so he holds on to his place at all costs.

The inability to own does not mitigate the competition for position. She embitters her.

**Patronage.** The project has four thousand places. Who do they take there? The right to give away seats is a currency, and in a system without capital it becomes the main currency. Hence the clientele, mutual responsibility, the exchange “I take your man - you support my tranche.”

**Reciprocity between projects.** I support your tranche, you mine. Legally indistinguishable from independent good faith decisions, because decisions can indeed be sincere. There is nothing to check: the “interesting” criterion cannot be falsified.

---

#III. Castle: no one will draw the border because the referee is prohibited

You yourself named the exception - “unless you initially do a project like build me a castle.” This exception is the whole problem.

There is a continuum between “a castle for yourself” and “a research campus with excellent team conditions”:

- housing for employees at the project site;
- good housing for employees;
- the manager’s house is near the site, because it must be available around the clock;
- representative building for receiving partners;
- transport, because the site is far away;
- helicopter, because the site is very far away.

Every step is justified. Only those who have the authority to decide what is excessive can draw the line. It is a discretionary body and is prohibited by principle. Transparency does not help here: all costs are visible, the dispute is not about facts, but about relevance - and relevance is not inferred from the data (see file 10, section II).

---

#IV. With a status incentive, the rational strategy is not theft, but empire building

The philosophical core of your concept directly preserves the desire for recognition, influence, and implementation of your ideas. This means that the reward is **the very fact of managing a large project**. Then there is no need to steal - leadership is the prize.

Hence the rational strategy of the participant: to maximize the size of the project, its duration and the number of employees. Not to appropriate, but to grow.

**Why is it worse than theft, and not better:**

- theft is limited to what can be hidden; the growth of the project is not limited by anything and looks like an achievement;
- theft is condemned and therefore underground; expansion of the project is public and approved;
- theft has a victim who can complain; excess growth has no victim - only unbuilt alternatives that no one sees (counterfactual problem from file 10);
- theft stops when caught; growth stops only when funding is withdrawn, and the withdrawal rests on M4 and the default dilemma from File 11.

This, by the way, is exactly what the Soviet ministry did: it did not steal, but demanded more resources than necessary, because the size of the department was the only currency. Hence the systematic overestimation of applications and the accumulation of reserves “just in case” - a mechanism that Kornai dismantled as an insatiable investment hunger.

**Result of this section:** you eliminated the monetary incentive and left the status incentive, declaring it a virtue. But the status incentive in the management of public resources does not give rise to frugality, but to gigantomania, and it is legitimate, public and unlimited.

---

# V. “You don’t have to invest at all” - the hardest part

Here your observation is accurate, and that is why it is dangerous. Let's look at what it means to have no obligation to invest.

**What profit does in the current system.** Not only distorts - it **forces savings to turn into capital**, without anyone's command. Money lying idle loses value; invested - bring. Therefore, the aggregate rate of accumulation is kept high without anyone setting it.

**What happens when there is no return.** Contributing resources to a project becomes a pure act of interest or altruism. And we have a measured base rate for such acts.

| | GDP share |
|---|---|
| Gross fixed capital formation (US) | about 21% |
| Gross capital formation in the world | about 25% |
| Charity in the United States, the most generous of rich countries | about 2% |

**The gap is an order of magnitude.** Voluntary channeling of funds without expectation of return is something we are already seeing, and it produces about 2% of GDP, not 20%.

**Why this is a free-rider problem, not a greed problem.** Society's capital stock is a public good: everyone uses it, my personal contribution to it is negligible, and it will not suffer noticeably from my refusal. Everyone's rational strategy is to consume their share and take advantage of what others have built. This is Olson applied to the most important aggregate of the economy, and there is not a single mechanism in your system that would compensate for it: no interest rate, no tax, no mandatory deduction.

**Possible objection and why it does not save.** You will say: the needs are satisfied, there is nowhere else to consume, there is nowhere to put the money except for projects. But consumption beyond the basic has no ceiling: services, impressions, travel, personal attention from other people, rare things, status consumption. And most importantly, there is a third option, which dominates for an inattentive person: **just save**. Saving does not require effort, does not require understanding reports and cannot result in embarrassment from failure.

And then the accumulated balances are the cash overhang from file 08. We come to the same place from the other side: the system encourages holding demands, and not turning them into capacities.

**Problem Statement:** Your design transforms **capital accumulation** from an automatic consequence of private interest into a **voluntary collective action**. All known voluntary collective actions of this magnitude are on the order of 2%, not 20%. This is not configurable by parameters: the only known ways to raise the accumulation rate are coercion (tax, mandatory deduction) or reward (interest, profit), and both are prohibited by design.

I would put this point in second place in strength after delegation (M4/U4) - and it directly follows from the property that you called the main one.

---

#VI. “Interesting” as a distribution criterion

You formulate a rule: give when the project is interesting. This is a fair description of the mechanism, but note how “interesting” differs from “needed”.

The sewer system is not interesting. Maintaining a worn-out bridge is not fun. Surveillance of bacterial resistance to antibiotics is not interesting. Replacing transformers at substations is not interesting. None of these problems will ever be interesting, no matter how much transparency and education there is - not because people are not conscientious enough, but because interest by its nature is attracted by novelty and visibility, and these are neither of these by definition.

This brings us back to question 4 and the distribution of streamer income from file 10: the mechanism gives a power-law distribution of attention. The rule “I give when it’s interesting” does not answer the objection, but formulates it.

---

#VII. What really goes away with the profit is an honest asset

So that the analysis is not one-sided. Along with the profit motive, real pathologies disappear, and they are not small:

- planned obsolescence and deliberate complication of repairs;
- patent thickets and retention of technologies for the sake of rent, and not for the sake of development;
- marketing that creates a need that did not exist;
- financialization: repurchase of own shares instead of investments in production;
- advertising in the amount of about 1% of world GDP;
- the pressure of quarterly reporting, cutting long programs;
- insurance medicine, where refusal to pay is a source of profit.

This is a real win, and it is not canceled out by anything written above. The correct wording of the argument is: **you are trading a set of distortions for the absence of a mechanism.** Distortions are expensive and disgusting; the absence of a mechanism is not a distortion, but an empty space, and cannot be assessed in the same units.

---

# VIII. Bottom line

| Your statement | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Stock assignment blocked | **Correct.** The "take out and own" scheme doesn't work |
| This means there will be no assignment | **False.** Appropriated by flow: consumption through position, patronage, reciprocity. This is a documented model of non-proprietary systems |
| You can't build a castle for yourself | **Formally yes, substantively no.** The castle is designated as a service castle. And tying a benefit to a position toughens the struggle for position, rather than softening it |
| No motive to profit | **True, but superseded.** The status incentive is declared the main one, and it provides a project growth strategy that is public, legitimate and unlimited |
| You don't have to invest at all | **True and destructive.** Capital accumulation becomes a voluntary collective action; the observed base rate for such actions is about 2% of GDP versus the required 20% |
| We give when it's interesting | **True as a description.** “Interesting” ≠ “needed” and this is a reframing of the problem, not a solution |
