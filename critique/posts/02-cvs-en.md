## Title

**Try to break this: social dividend + citizen-directed investment, no central plan, no private ownership of capital. I'll list the objections I already have.**

## Body

I am not here to defend this. I want the strongest reasons it fails. To save everyone time, I've listed the objections I already know at the bottom — please don't repeat those, beat them.

**The system.** Assume high automation. Basic needs are met unconditionally. Means of production cannot be privately owned. Every citizen receives a recurring share of social product; unspent share accumulates in a personal balance. That balance grants no ownership of factories or land, but the holder directs it: anyone can propose a project ("new engine type, 10bn units"), and it gets funded if enough citizens send their balances to it. People can also join projects with their labour. Ordinary production follows where resources and labour go. Long-term projects can get multi-year pledges, revocable as information comes in. Strategic reserves are set by citizens, with experts and AI advising but not deciding. No standing body sets priorities. Rules change only by citizen decision. The army is professional, may act without a vote under attack, and has no independent economic base.

**Objections I already hold — don't repost these, break them or go past them:**

1. **Calculation.** The dividend gives you consumer-side signals. Mises' actual target was capital-goods allocation between competing techniques. With no owner staking a non-replenishing endowment, capital-goods prices are either administered (= the banned central body) or noise.
2. **Soft budget constraint.** A citizen risks a *flow* that comes back next month; a capitalist risks a *stock* that doesn't. So the expected cost of funding garbage is near zero, and selection pressure collapses. Kornai without a planner to at least try balancing.
3. **Nobody monitors.** 1/N of the oversight benefit, 100% of the cost. Insiders end up controlling the assets they don't own — Djilas' new class, Berle–Means taken to the limit. Note that Roemer, proposing almost this exact coupon scheme in 1994, had to bolt on main banks as monitors, and Schweickart routed investment through public regional banks. Both added the institution this design bans.
4. **Delegation.** Thousands of projects, finite attention ⇒ people delegate ⇒ curators ⇒ power-law concentration of directed capital. Pirate Party LiquidFeedback superdelegates; DAO delegate concentration; index funds holding ~20% of S&P 500 voting power while "owning" nothing.
5. **Beauty contest.** Threshold funding makes the question "will others fund this", not "is this good". Cascades and fads. And nobody can profit by debunking an overhyped project, so no professional sceptic exists (Grossman–Stiglitz). Marketing becomes the highest-return activity there is.
6. **Unpopular but necessary.** Sewers, vector control, TB surveillance, bridge maintenance. Diffuse benefit, no narrative, no funding.
7. **Long horizons.** Revocable pledges kill 20-year projects, which look like failures at year 7 by construction. Irrevocable pledges = an unaccountable permanent fund = the banned body.
8. **McKelvey.** "Citizens set priorities" over a multidimensional space has generically no stable core; whoever writes the ballot question decides. "No body sets priorities" doesn't remove the agenda setter, it just makes them unaccountable.
9. **Olson.** A coordinated 2% beats an uncoordinated 60%, always, and here they don't even need to buy a politician — they just coordinate their own dividends.
10. **Nobody runs the existing factories.** The design explains funding *new* projects and says nothing about who decides capital replacement, hiring, or suppliers at a running plant. Options: worker collectives (Yugoslavia: Ward–Domar–Vanek perverse supply, Furubotn–Pejovich horizon problem), appointed managers (banned body), or nobody.
11. **Army.** A professional army as the only permanent disciplined hierarchy in a society with no professional political class is the classic praetorian setup (Finer, Nordlinger). And "acts without a vote when attacked" gives command the power to declare the exception — that's Schmitt's definition of sovereignty.
12. **Convertibility leaks.** Gifts, paying for services, reciprocal funding rings, secondary markets, inheritance. Every non-convertible parallel currency in history (Beryozka certificates, CUC, wartime ration coupons) grew a secondary market within months. Plugging the leaks requires total transaction surveillance.

**What I actually want.** A mechanism I haven't listed, in the form "X happens → agents get an incentive to do Y → Z results → system destabilises." Historical counterexamples especially welcome. And most of all: an objection that **cannot be fixed by tuning parameters** — not "the threshold is wrong", but "these two requirements are logically incompatible."
