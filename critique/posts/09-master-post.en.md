# EN

## Title options

- **A society that funds itself voluntarily: where does it break?** (general)
- **Coupon socialism with no monitors and no compulsory levy — attack the two numbers** (LessWrong, r/badeconomics)
- **Ask HN: what sustains ~15% of GDP in maintenance without either profit or a mandatory levy?** (HN)

## Body

I want this broken, not improved. I've done thirteen rounds of self-critique already, so I've listed below what has already fallen and what I think the real blockers are. Repeating the fallen ones costs us both time; beating the blockers, or naming one I've missed, is what I'm here for.

### The design

The goal is **not** capital accumulation or growth. It is maintaining a working society and developing it.

Assume high automation and that basic needs are met unconditionally. Means of production are socially owned and cannot be privately held. There is no central planning body. Every citizen receives a recurring individual share of social product; unspent share accumulates in a personal balance conferring no ownership of productive assets, but the holder directs it.

Anyone may propose a project with a stated cost and work plan. **Funding is released in tranches against progress reports, not as a lump sum** — nobody hands over 10bn at once. Citizens may also join with labour. There is **no obligation to invest**: with no private profit, you direct resources to a project only if it genuinely interests you.

Prices are standardised — three units spent means three units asked.

Ordinary production follows where resources and labour go; transparency substitutes for external control, since a factory making bearings nobody takes is visible to everyone including its own workers, and nobody has a private motive to prop it up.

Long-horizon projects can receive multi-year pledges, revisable as information arrives. Strategic reserves are set by citizens; experts and AI advise but never decide. Every allocation is traceable: citizen decision → operative rule → source data → calculation → distribution. The army is professional and voluntary, may act without a vote under direct attack, and has no independent economic base.

**Governing principle:** maintenance and development without coercion organised by people — no compulsory levies, no assigned quotas, no body deciding for others.

### Objections that have already failed — please don't repost these

1. **"Markets discipline capital allocation by selecting out bad investors."** Weak empirically. ~6% of VC deals produce ~60% of industry returns, so you can be wrong nine times in ten and succeed. Wealth buffers selection pressure in proportion to its size. Zombie firms reached roughly 15% of listed firms in advanced economies. Inheritance severs the link between decision quality and resources controlled every generation. And 2010–2021 was a decade of near-absent discipline in which the economy nonetheless functioned. "No selection ⇒ collapse" is refuted.
2. **"Transparency can't replace the price signal."** For ordinary reproducible goods it can: unsold inventory is a cheap, unambiguous, fast, attributable signal. Allocator selection is redundant there.
3. **"Insiders will keep failed projects alive."** Tranches invert the default: money doesn't move unless someone acts. Continuation now requires effort; stopping is free.
4. **"The dividend causes inflation."** Not with standardised prices. The problem persists in another form (see blockers), but not as inflation.
5. **"Strategic reserves need a permanent agency."** Agencies with statutory mandates and protected budgets existed and failed: pandemic plans were near-universal in 2019, adequate stockpiles were not. "Give it to mandated professionals" is refuted by the mandated professionals.
6. **"The calculation problem."** Largely inapplicable to *maintenance*, which is technically determinable — transformer service life is an engineering standard, not a price. It bites on *development*, not upkeep.
7. **"Inequality returns via inheritance."** This one favours the design: dynastic inheritance currently severs the link between allocative skill and resources entirely.

### What I think the real blockers are — beat these

**B1. The maintenance rate: ~15% vs ~2%.** Refusing growth does not make the requirement small. Consumption of fixed capital — literally the cost of keeping things from degrading — runs about 15–16% of GDP in developed economies. Gross fixed capital formation in the US is ~21%, of which only ~5% is net addition. So "no growth, just upkeep" still means roughly a sixth of social product. The observed rate of voluntary giving with no expected return, in the most generous rich country, is about 2% of GDP. That is an order of magnitude, and it is a free-rider problem rather than a greed problem: my contribution to the capital stock is negligible, and simply hoarding dominates for anyone inattentive. The only two known ways to sustain a high rate are compulsion or reward, and the design bans both. **The single most useful thing anyone can give me: a case where voluntary, return-free allocation sustained even 8–10% of GDP.** I know of none.

**B2. Delegation concentrates, and tranches make it worse.** A 12-year, 10bn project at quarterly tranches is ~48 decisions. At 20 projects per person that's 80 technical progress reports a year, each in a different specialty. That is a part-time job, so people delegate, and delegate weight goes power-law — LiquidFeedback superdelegates, DAO delegate concentration, three index funds holding ~20% of S&P 500 votes. Banning delegation demands the impossible; allowing it concentrates; capping it is evaded by recommendation lists, and banning those means banning published opinion.

**B3. Latent infrastructure has no floor.** Local visible infrastructure is arguably *better* under this design (Porto Alegre's participatory budget genuinely improved water and sewerage in poor districts). But structural reserves, transformer replacement, resistance surveillance: today's floor is low but nonzero, held by legal service obligations, automatic tariff revenue, and personal liability — none of which is enthusiasm, and none of which exists here. The sharpest counterexample is condo associations: they run on a *mandatory* levy, their signature failure is deferred maintenance, Champlain Towers South collapsed in 2021 after years of owners resisting a special assessment, and Florida responded by legally removing owners' right to vote down structural reserves. That is the most favourable possible case — bounded group, personally visible defects, hours of feedback latency — and it still needs compulsion.

**B4. The default between tranches.** Default-continue restores inertia. Default-stop makes projects re-win attention quarterly, so the memorable survive rather than the good — and a 20-year project must run a 20-year publicity campaign with nothing to show. Both ends are bad; intermediate settings are points on the segment between them, not a solution.

**B5. Nobody runs existing enterprises.** The design covers funding *new* projects and is silent on who decides capital replacement, hiring, and suppliers at a running plant. Worker collectives (Yugoslav pathologies), appointed managers (the banned body), or nobody. This is a gap, not a trade-off.

**B6. The agenda setter is ineliminable.** "Citizens set reserve priorities" over a multidimensional space has generically no stable core (McKelvey), so whoever sequences the questions drives the outcome. Someone must write the ballot, define "adequate", contract the supplier, rotate stock. Denying that body exists doesn't remove it, it removes its accountability.

**B7. Civilian control of the army has never been exercised by citizens directly.** It is exercised by a *competing professional hierarchy* — a civilian defence ministry, a legislature with budget power. The design removes the second hierarchy and keeps the first. Also, "acts without a vote when attacked" gives command the power to declare that an attack has occurred.

**B8. Status is the stated motive, so the rational strategy is empire-building, not theft.** You've blocked accumulating a stolen stock — genuinely. But where ownership is impossible, appropriation historically runs through the office: assigned housing, transport, special supply. And because the perk dies with the post, holders fight *harder* to keep the post than an owner who can sell and leave.

### One structural note, in case it saves someone time

B1, B3, B4, B6 and B7 are the same refusal repeated. Each of those problems is currently held by an institution whose defining feature is that it is **deliberately not subject to the current will of the majority**: a compulsory levy doesn't ask for enthusiasm; a charter binds a future majority; a defence ministry is a rival hierarchy. The design's governing principle is the negation of exactly that property. So they can't be patched one at a time — the decision is made once, at the level of the principle.

On the foundation, to pre-empt a common exchange: this is not a *ban* on ownership. No one can build a factory alone; where others helped, an exclusive claim to the result has no ground. Land was produced by nobody and Locke's proviso — "enough and as good left in common" — has long failed. Knowledge is cumulative, and simultaneous discovery is the rule rather than the exception. Herbert Simon put the share of income in a rich country attributable to social inheritance at roughly 90%.

Granting all of that, though, the enforcement question is separate from the justification question. The absence of a valid exclusive claim does not dissolve *conflicts over use*: two projects want the same riverside site; one crew wants to keep the line running, another to repurpose the shop. If society doesn't adjudicate, exclusive control exists in fact regardless of its lack of moral basis; if it does, that is organised enforcement. Common ownership is the statement of the problem, not its solution — which is Ostrom's central point: working commons differ from destroyed ones by having boundaries, monitoring and sanctions, not by being common.

### What I'm asking for

Mechanisms in the form: **X happens → agents' best response is Y → Z follows → the system destabilises.** Historical counterexamples above all. And most valuable of all: an objection that survives any parameter setting, or the empirical case for B1 that I couldn't find.

## Body

I don't need confirmation that the idea is good. I went through thirteen rounds of self-criticism, so below I separately list what has already fallen away and what I consider to be real blockers. To repeat what has fallen away is to waste the time of both; It's valuable to either break the blocker or name the one I missed.

### Construction

The goal is **not** capital accumulation and not growth. The goal is to maintain a functioning society and its development.

High automation, basic needs are provided unconditionally. The means of production belong to society and cannot be privately owned. There is no planning body. Everyone receives a regular individual share of the social product; the unspent amount accumulates in a personal account, does not give the right to own factories, but the citizen disposes of it himself.

Anyone can propose a project with a cost and work plan. **Financing comes in tranches according to reports, and not as a one-time amount** - no one gives 10 billion at once. You can join by work. **There is no obligation to invest**: there is no private profit, so the funds are sent where and only where the sender is really interested.

Prices are standardized: spent three units - they ask for three units.

Conventional production follows where resources and labor go; transparency replaces external control, because a factory making bearings that no one needs is visible to everyone, including its own employees, and no one has a personal motive to support pointless work.

Long-term projects receive multi-year commitments, with revisions as information becomes available. Strategic reserves are established by citizens; Experts and AI advise, but do not decide. Any distribution can be verified along the chain decision → rule → data → calculation → output. The army is professional and voluntary, acts without voting when attacked, and has no economic base of its own.

**Principle:** maintenance and development without coercion, organized by people - without mandatory contributions, without assigned tasks, without a body deciding for others.

### Objections that have already failed - do not repeat them

1. **“The market disciplines the selection of investors.”** Empirically weak: about 6% of venture transactions provide about 60% of the industry’s profitability, that is, you can be wrong in nine cases out of ten and be successful; richness dampens selection pressure in proportion to size; zombies reached about 15% of public companies; inheritance breaks the connection between the quality of decisions and resources at each generation. And 2010–2021 is a decade of almost disconnected discipline, the economy worked. The conclusion “collapse without selection” is refuted.
2. **“Transparency will not replace price.”** For regular repeatable products, it replaces: unsold stock - a cheap, unambiguous, fast and attributable signal.
3. **“They won’t let us close a failed project.”** Tranches reverse the silence: the money doesn’t flow until no one has completed the action.
4. **“The dividend will cause inflation.”** With standardized prices, no. The problem remains in a different form, see blockers.
5. **“Reserves should be handled by a permanent body.”** A body with a statutory mandate and a protected budget existed and failed: pandemic plans in 2019 were almost everywhere, reserves were almost nowhere.
6. **“Calculation problem.”** Almost inapplicable to **maintenance**: transformer service life is an engineering standard, not a price. It hits **development**, not service.
7. **“Inequality will return through inheritance.”** Point in favor of the design: now dynastic inheritance breaks the connection between the quality of decisions and resources completely.

### Real blockers - hit here

**B1. Maintenance Rate: ~15% vs. ~2%.** Not growing does not make the requirement small. Consumption of fixed capital - literally the price of not degrading anything - is about 15-16% of GDP in developed economies. Gross capital formation in the United States is about 21%, of which the net increase is about 5%. That is, “no growth, only maintenance” is still a sixth of the social product. The observed rate of voluntary transfer of funds without expectation of return, in the most generous of rich countries, is about 2% of GDP. The gap is an order of magnitude, and this is free riding, not greed: my contribution to the total capital stock is negligible, and for the inattentive, the strategy of simply accumulating is dominant. There are exactly two known ways to maintain a high standard - coercion or reward - and the design prohibits both. **The most useful thing you can give me is: a case where the voluntary non-refundable allocation of funds consistently provided at least 8-10% of GDP.** I don’t know of any of these.

**B2. Delegation concentrates, tranches enhance this.** A project for 10 billion with a horizon of 12 years with quarterly tranches - about 48 decisions. With 20 projects per person, this is 80 technical reports per year, each in their own specialty. This is a side hustle, so they delegate, and the weight of the delegates becomes sedate: LiquidFeedback superdelegates, concentration in the DAO, three index funds with about 20% of the S&P 500 voting power.

**B3. Hidden infrastructure has no floor.** Local visible infrastructure is probably **better** with this design (Porto Alegre has actually improved water and sanitation in poor areas). But constructive reserves, transformer replacement, resistance supervision: today's floor is low, but not zero, and is kept in place by legal duty of service, automatic tariff and personal responsibility - none is enthusiastic and none is here. A stark counterexample is condo associations: holding on to a **required** assessment, being denied through deferred repairs, Champlain Towers South collapsed in 2021 after years of owner resistance to a special assessment, and Florida responded by legislating the right to vote to waive the reserve. This is the most favorable case - a small group, the defect is visible in person, feedback is on the clock - and coercion is still required.

**B4. Silence between tranches.** “Continue” - inertia returns. “Stop” - the project re-gains attention every quarter, the memorable ones survive, not the good ones, and a twenty-year project needs a twenty-year campaign with no results. Both ends are bad.

**B5. No one manages the existing enterprise.** Financing of new projects is described; who decides about replacing a machine, hiring and a supplier at a working factory - no. The work collective (Yugoslav pathologies), the appointed manager (prohibited body) or no one. This is a gap, not a compromise.

**B6. The setter agenda is irreducible.** In a multidimensional space and a majority rule, the core is usually empty (McKelvey), and the questioner determines the outcome. Someone formulates a question, determines “sufficient volume,” selects a supplier, and rotates the warehouse.

**B7. Civilian control of the army has never been exercised directly by citizens.** It is exercised by a **competing professional hierarchy**. The design removes the second hierarchy and leaves the first. And “acts without voting during an attack” gives the command the right to determine the fact of an attack.

**B8. Status is declared as an incentive, so the rational strategy is not theft, but expansion.** It is really impossible to accumulate stolen stock. But where it is impossible to own, they appropriate it through a position: official housing, transport, supplies. And since the good dies along with the post, the post is held on more tightly than the owner, who can sell and leave.

### Structural note

B1, B3, B4, B6, B7 - the same failure. Each of these tasks today is carried out by an institution, the defining property of which is that it is **deliberately not subordinate to the current will of the majority**: compulsory expulsion does not require enthusiasm, the charter binds the future majority, the Ministry of Defense is a competing hierarchy. The design principle is the negation of exactly this property. This means you can’t fix things one at a time—the decision is made once, at the level of principle.

About the reason not to waste a thread on this: it is **not a ban** to own. The plant cannot be built alone; Since there was help from others, the exclusive claim to the result is unfounded. No one produced the earth, and Locke’s clause—“there remains enough of the same quality for others”—has not been fulfilled for a long time. Knowledge is cumulative, and simultaneity of discoveries is the rule, not the exception. Herbert Simon estimated the share of income in a rich country that comes from social heritage to be about 90%.

But if this is fully accepted, the question of execution remains separate from the question of justification. Lack of a valid exclusive claim does not eliminate **use conflicts**: two projects want one river site; one team wants to maintain the line, the other wants to repurpose the workshop. If society does not resolve such disputes, exclusive control arises in fact, regardless of its lack of moral basis; if it allows, it is organized coercion with the performer. The commonality of a resource is the formulation of a problem, not its solution: working commons differ from destroyed ones by the presence of boundaries, monitoring and sanctions, and not by the presence of commonality (Ostrom’s main thesis).

### What exactly am I asking for?

Mechanisms in the form: **X occurs → agents' optimal response Y → Z occurs → the system loses stability.** First of all, historical counterexamples. And the most valuable thing is an objection that survives any parameter setting, or an empiricist according to B1, which I did not find.
