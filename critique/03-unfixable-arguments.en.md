# Part 3. Arguments that cannot be eliminated by setting parameters

Selection criterion: the objection remains valid for **any** parameter values ​​(dividend size, thresholds, terms, matching share, delegation rules), because it follows from the logical incompatibility of the requirements, from a mathematical theorem, or from a limitation built into the physics of attention and time.

If you want to defend the concept, these are the points you need to defend. The rest is tuning.

---

##U1. Incompatibility: “no residual bidder” + “informative capital prices”

The price of a capital good carries information only because someone places a non-renewable supply of one's own against one's own valuation. Remove the bettor - either the administrator (banned) or noise will remain. It's not "hard to implement", it's a contradiction in definitions.

*Possible answer:* allow firms with residual income from labor collectives. Then you get market socialism of the Yugoslav type with its list of problems (M7), but you lose the thesis “the means of production belong to society” in a meaningful sense.

---

##U2. Incompatibility: “citizens set priorities” + “there is no body that determines priorities”

McKelvey's Chaos Theorem: In a multidimensional space of alternatives under a majority rule, the core is usually empty, and the controller of the voting sequence can lead the outcome almost anywhere. This means that the agent-setter always exists and always has decisive power. Denying its existence does not eliminate it, but deprives it of accountability.

*Cannot be bypassed:* by sorting (the chamber of lots is the body), by the algorithm (whoever writes the algorithm is the body), by AI (see U7).

---

##U3. Incompatibility: “financing is withdrawn at any time” + “projects with a horizon of 20 years”

The problem of time inconsistency. An ex ante optimal commitment is no longer an ex post optimal commitment, so a renegotiable mechanism cannot support long commitments. The only known cure is tying hands (irrevocable fund, constitutional norm), that is, the creation of an institution beyond the control of citizens.

---

##U4. Attention deficit ⇒ delegation ⇒ concentration

The number of decisions requiring evaluation grows along with the economy; A citizen's time is constant. Delegation is inevitable; the returns to specialization in attention are positive and cumulative; the distribution of delegated shares becomes power-law. Restrictions on delegation are circumvented by informal “lists of recommendations”, which to prohibit = prohibit the publication of opinions.

This is Michels' iron law, derived not from psychology, but from the arithmetic of time.

---

##U5. Olson: asymmetry of concentrated benefits and dispersed costs

No known institution addresses this asymmetry; it can only be partially compensated for (professional bureaucracy with a mandate, judicial control, competing branches of government). The concept abolishes all these compensators as a “professional political class.”

---

##U6. The problem of monitoring under diffuse ownership has exactly two solutions, and both are prohibited

Concentrated share or delegated supervisory authority. The absence of both does not mean "no control by a few", but **control by insiders with no outside control at all**. Djilas, Burnham, Berle and Means describe this outcome using three different materials.

---

##U7. General AI advisor breaks the independence condition in Condorcet's theorem

The epistemic justification for mass decision making rests on the independence of errors. The general advisor model introduces a general error component. The more useful and widespread the EA, the more strongly correlated the judgments are, and the weaker the “many people will be wrong in different directions” argument. This is the mathematics of aggregation, not a property of a particular model.

*Walking through "many different models"* only works as long as the models are truly independent - and they are trained on overlapping data, evaluated on overlapping benchmarks, and tuned to overlapping norms.

---

##U8. Adverse selection under equal division and free exit

If the share does not depend on the contribution, and exit is possible, then, for any distribution of abilities, those who invest more than the average have a positive incentive to exit, and entry is beneficial to those who invest less than the average. The threshold at which this does not destroy the system is set not by the parameters of the scheme, but by external options and the strength of ideology. The kibbutzim are a direct empirical illustration: the system worked for generations and fell apart when external options grew.

*The only known exceptions* are closed communities with high exit costs and strict internal controls (Amish, Hutterites, monasteries). It's not something you design.

---

##U9. Transparency requires an apparatus; the apparatus is a bureaucracy

Your own traceability requirement “decision → rule → data → calculation → distribution” is a technical specification for a permanent administrative organization: standards, registers, audit, dispute resolution, anti-fraud, identification. Refusal to call it an authority does not eliminate it, but removes it from formal responsibility. This is according to Weber, not according to Marx.

---

##U10. Rejection of the constitutional level makes the horizon of rules shorter than the horizon of projects

If rules are changed by the same procedure and with the same ease as operational decisions, then the expected life of any rule is limited, and a rational agent will not commit to anything longer than this time. The clause about “rules can only be changed by the decision of citizens” without an increased threshold directly kills the clause about long-term projects.

---

##U11. Emergency mode is a built-in unidirectional transition

War requires directive reallocation. Any structure that contains the “in an emergency situation we act in a directive” mode also contains a path from a decentralized state to a centralized one. Reversion is not automatic: it requires someone to voluntarily relinquish authority. The historical baseline for such refusal is low (Higgs, “Crisis and Leviathan”).

---

##U13. Allocator selection: disabled versus absent mechanism

**Phrasing revised and weakened** following objection to power-law distribution of venture capital returns and zombie companies. Full analysis of the markdown - [09-how-strong-is-selection-really.md](09-how-strong-is-selection-really.md).

**Which is cancelled.** The statement “the market corrects mistakes by depriving erring people of capital” describes an idealized, not an observed, market. Deal-by-deal selection does not work (about 6% of transactions provide about 60% of the industry’s profitability); wealth dampens selection pressure the more strongly, the greater it is; the share of zombies among public companies in developed countries reached approximately 15%; inheritance breaks the connection between the quality of decisions and the amount of resources at each generation. The conclusion “no selection ⇒ the system falls apart” is refuted by the era of 2010–2021: there was almost no discipline, the economy was functioning.

**What remains.** Three narrow statements:

1. **A switched-off mechanism versus an absent one.** The selection coefficient in capitalism is low, but recoverable: the rise in capital prices in 2022–2024 killed an entire cohort of unprofitable companies. In concept, it is structurally zero, and there is no switch - no rate, no creditor, no moment of claim.
2. **Gender that does not exist.** The zombie lives because the creditor tolerates her, and tolerance is revoked. In the concept, there is not a single participant whose tolerance could be revoked, because no one expected a return in the first place.
3. **A professional skeptic exists only if there is a claim for a return.** During the decade when shorting was unprofitable, undetected fraud increased. The concept makes this loss constant and constructive, rather than cyclical.

**Third edition after the objection about transparency** (full analysis - [10-transparency-as-substitute-for-selection.md](10-transparency-as-substitute-for-selection.md)): for ordinary repeatable goods with observable demand, transparency replaces selection **completely** - the signal is cheap, unambiguous, fast and attributable, and in this segment the concept is workable. This clause applies only to projects whose outcome does not yet exist, whose metric is disputed, or whose evaluation requires comparison with an unrealized alternative. There, transparency provides data, but does not give a signal to stop, and the removal of monetary benefits inverts the structure of coalitions: the “continue” side (those involved in the project, its management, publicly committed sponsors) is concentrated and organized, the “stop” side is dispersed. In capitalism it’s the other way around, and that’s why the stop occurs.

**Why is this still not configurable with the parameter:** The selection switch can only be restored by entering a return claim, that is, capital.

**Realistic forecast instead of “the system will collapse”:** the quality of resource allocation at the level of the philanthropic and grant sector, applied to the entire economy, is workable, with a steady shift towards the noticeable, with fashions and without a signal about their end.

---

##U14. Capital accumulation becomes a voluntary collective action

Added after the objection “there is no investor, there is no need to make money, you don’t have to invest at all” - a full analysis in [12-no-investor-no-profit-motive.md](12-no-investor-no-profit-motive.md), section V.

Profit performs a function that no one inherits in the design: it **forcibly transforms savings into production capacity** - without a command, without a plan and without an authority. What lies without movement depreciates, what is invested brings value; Therefore, the accumulation rate remains high, although no one prescribes it.

Take away the return, and directing resources to a project becomes a pure act of interest. The observed base rate for such acts has been measured at about 2% of GDP in the United States, the most generous of rich countries. The required gross fixed capital formation is about 21% of GDP in the USA, about 25% in the world. **Gap by an order of magnitude.**

This is a free-rider problem, not a greed problem: society's capital stock is a public good, individual contributions to it are negligible, and the rational strategy is to consume one's share and benefit from what others have built. The third option for an inattentive person dominates both: **just save**, which requires no effort, no analysis of reports, no risk of embarrassment from failure - and gives a monetary overhang from file 08.

**Why it can’t be configured:** There are exactly two known ways to increase the savings rate - coercion (tax, mandatory deductions) or reward (interest, profit). The concept prohibits both.

In terms of strength, I put this point second after U4 (delegation → concentration), and it follows directly from the property that the author of the concept calls its main advantage.

---

##U12. Category note: concept describes distribution, not production

The whole structure is a mechanism for distributing the right to direct resources. Not a single element of it answers the question of who and how makes thousands of daily production decisions at existing facilities. This is not a gap in the narrative, but the missing half of the economic system. Any critical economist will be the first to notice this.

---

## What would be an honest way to save the idea

Not as advice for improvement, but as an indication of what it becomes when contradictions are eliminated:

1. Recognize the existence of permanent bodies (funds with a mandate, supervision, statistics, anti-fraud) and engage in their accountability instead of denying them.
2. Recognize the constitutional level with an increased threshold for change.
3. Recognize residual contenders at the enterprise level (cooperatives) and, therefore, the market for capital goods.
4. Recognize delegation and design it (limits, transparency, recall, conflict of interest), rather than prohibit it.
5. Limit the direct civilian distribution of shares of the social product to the segment where it empirically works: local, observable, short-term projects - that is, a participatory budget on a city scale.

After these five steps, what remains is: social democracy with a strong UBI, a cooperative sector, public investment funds and a participatory budget at the local level. This is an already existing, discussed and partially tested position - and it is certainly stronger than the original formulation. But it does not contain what made the original idea attractive: the absence of permanent institutions of power.
