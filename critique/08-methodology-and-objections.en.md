# I. What parameters were used to compare

The complaint is fair: in file 07 the criteria were not named. I call it.

**Base of comparison:** rich mixed economy of the 2020s (US/EU) - not idealized textbook capitalism, not a poor country. This is a choice, and it affects the outcome: when compared to an idealized market, the concept would look better, when compared to the average country in the world, it would also look better.

**Four parameters based on which the verdict was given:**

1. **Does a mechanism exist at all?** It is not “assumed that citizens will decide,” but a specific procedure: who, on what basis, in what time frame, with what consequences in case of refusal. The declaration is a zero on this parameter, no matter how reasonable it is.
2. **Has the mechanism been observed at work on a comparable scale?** The city's participatory budget for 1.5 million people has been observed. Distribution of the country's entire capital by voting - no.
3. **Resilient to low participation and to dishonest participants.** What happens with 5% active and with a coordinated group of 2%.
4. **Feedback speed.** How long does it take for an error to become visible to someone who can fix it.

**What this score does NOT and cannot measure:**

- fairness of distribution;
- freedom from coercion in the workplace;
- alienation;
- dignity.

According to these parameters, the concept wins, and I noted this (three ✅ in file 07). **Therefore, “7 is worse, 8 is worse, 5 is par, 3 is better” is not a judgment on the concept.** It is a statement of the form: in terms of mechanical performance, it loses in 15 areas out of 23, but wins in 3 areas, which for many people are more important than all the others combined. Weighing these groups is a value choice, and you make it, not me. My account is an entry into this solution, not a replacement for it.

**What the account still firmly states:** in seven areas (“⛔”) it is not about the fact that the mechanism is weaker, but about the fact that there is no mechanism and its introduction is prohibited by principle. It's not a question of scales. This is a question of whether the system will remain physically operational.

#II. “Prices are standardized, where does inflation come from?”

The objection is precise, and my wording in file 07 (paragraph 18) was inaccurate: I implicitly considered prices to be free. With fixed prices, there really will be no classical inflation. But the problem does not disappear - it changes form, and the new form is worse. I will analyze both possible meanings of “standardized prices”.

## Option A: prices are nominally fixed (“3 units are always 3 units”)

**What happens.** Number of units outstanding = dividend × population. Physical release varies independently. If, at a fixed price, demand exceeds supply, the price does not rise - the queue grows.

**Three consequences, each more serious than inflation:**

**1. Deficits instead of inflation.** This is exactly Kornai's scarcity economics. Distribution moves from price to other mechanisms: queue, recording, one-hand limit, familiarity with the distributor, priority list. All of them are opaque and all create rent for the one who is in charge of distribution. Note that this is exactly the type of power that the concept seeks to eliminate: power not through property, but through access.

**2. Money overhang - and you have it not as a side effect, but as a design requirement.** Your scheme **specifically** assumes that citizens accumulate large balances: this is the investment mechanism. This means that the system constantly has a supply of requirements for goods that is many times greater than the available goods. This is a monetary overhang.

It is dangerous because it is conditionally deferred. While everyone believes that the price will remain 3 units, the canopy is sleeping. At the moment when faith disappears - due to war, harvest, rumor, change of rule - the entire accumulated supply simultaneously comes to the counter. The historical example is direct: Soviet forced savings at fixed prices and the subsequent liberalization of prices in January 1992, which resulted in price increases of thousands of percent per year. The canopy did not disappear after 30 years of accumulation - it was realized in months.

**3. The signal that the dividend is too high disappears.** This is the most unpleasant thing. If prices are free, too large a dividend is immediately visible as a rise in prices - quickly, publicly, for everyone. With fixed prices, the excess is visible only as empty shelves, that is, with a delay, unevenly across goods and without a single number that can be presented at a vote. **Fixed prices do not solve the problem of an inflated dividend - they make it invisible.** The political incentive to vote for a higher dividend remains completely.

**And the main question you are avoiding is:** who sets these fixed prices? A new product has appeared - what is its price? Technology has reduced the cost of production by half - is the price changing? Someone must decide, and decide constantly, on millions of positions. This is either an organ (prohibited) or a formula - move on to option B.

## Option B: prices are calculated according to an objective standard (costs, labor hours)

This is the Cockshott-Cottrell approach: price = socially necessary labor costs. The problems are different and no less serious.

**1. The cost price does not contain information about demand.** A product that cost 3 units of labor and is not needed by anyone costs 3. A product that costs 3 and is needed by ten million costs 3. This means that the price does not tell production anything about what to make more of. The only remaining signal is the length of the queue. The queue is observable, but it is not comparable between goods and does not carry information about the intensity of desire: a line of one hundred people for bread and a line of one hundred people for a telescope look the same.

**2. Circle in definition.** Costs depend on the technology chosen. The choice of technology should depend on relative resource prices. Relative prices are calculated from costs. The circle closes, and it can only be opened by someone’s arbitrariness - that is, by the same organ.

**3. The zero price of the irreproducible is the most concrete consequence.** Land, field, area under the city, radio frequency, river flow, view from the window - all this does not contain labor. The cost price of such things is zero or is assigned arbitrarily. The result: systematic waste of precisely those resources that cannot be produced anew. This, by the way, is the point at which the Georgists will tear you apart - the concept has no theory of land and rent in general.

**4. Multidimensionality of quality.** “Boots” are not one product. Costly accounting collapses quality into a scalar and creates an incentive to fulfill the standard on the shaft: heavy nails instead of the necessary ones, meters of fabric instead of suitable fabric. This is not a joke, this is a persistent pathology of all systems with a designated indicator.

## Summary of prices

| | Free prices | Fixed | Costly |
|---|---|---|---|
| Inflation | there is | no | no |
| Shortage | no | there is | there is |
| Savings canopy | no | **yes, required by design** | there is |
| Signal “dividend is too high” | fast, public | missing | missing |
| Signal to production | price | queue | queue |
| The price of the irreproducible | there is | arbitrary | zero |
| Who installs | nobody | organ | formula + organ on edges |

**Conclusion:** your objection is correct in exactly one way: the word “inflation” will not appear in this scenario. But the problem, which I called inflation, is the discrepancy between the issued demands and the available goods, and it persists under any price option. Fixing prices does not eliminate discrepancies, but hides them and accumulates them. The verdict on point 18 in file 07 remains ⛔, but the wording should be read as follows: **not “there will be inflation”, but “there is no mechanism for coordinating the size of the dividend with the physical issue, and with fixed prices there is also no signal of mismatch”**.

#III. “This is a self-regulating process: stupid investments give problems, smart ones give growth, people learn”

This is the strongest of your objections, and it requires a precise answer, because this is actually how the market works. But it doesn't work for the reason you say.

## The main difference: the market does not teach, the market selects

When an investor makes a mistake, what happens is not “the investor got wiser.” Something else is happening: **he has less capital**. In the next round, his opinion weighs less - automatically, without his consent, without him realizing his mistake, even if he died. Capital flows to those whose past valuations have been justified.

This is selection, not training. Evolution requires three things: diversity, inheritance and **differential reproduction**. You have diversity, but differential reproduction is not there by design.

**Specifically.** I financed ten failures, you financed ten successes. What is our dividend next month? **Same.** My influence on the distribution of society's resources has not decreased one bit. The system didn't remember anything at the level where it matters.

This is, in my opinion, the purest irreducible argument in the entire set - stronger than Mises' calculus problem, because it does not require controversial assumptions:

> **You have a mutation without selection.** For the system to learn at the resource allocation level, the influence of successful allocators must increase, and that of unsuccessful ones must decrease. This is capital accumulation. By prohibiting accumulation, you prohibited the system from learning. Training individuals does not replace this - see below.

There is no intermediate option: any mechanism that increases the weight of a successful allocator is capital, and any refusal of it is a refusal of selection.

## Why training individuals is not a substitute for selection

**1. Investment decisions are an “evil” learning environment.** There are benevolent environments (quick, unambiguous feedback related to your action - chess, tennis) and evil ones (delay, noise, confused reasons). People learn only in the first. The distribution of capital is the second example: the result in 5–15 years, success depends on dozens of other people’s decisions, small samples, luck dominates skill.

**Fact check:** VCs have been doing this full time, on their own dime, for decades, with access to the best information - and the median fund doesn't beat the index, and consistent returns are concentrated in a handful of firms. If professionals with skin in the game barely learn, the occasional citizen will not learn.

**2. Learning is a reserve, and it evaporates with the change of generations.** Bubbles repeat approximately once a generation, despite all previous ones being fully documented and publicly available: 1929, 1987, 2000, 2008, 2021. The information was public each time. An institution is frozen knowledge that does not need to be relearned; The capital adequacy ratio also works for people who have never seen a banking panic. Take away the institutions, and each generation pays the full price of education anew.

**3. Mass retrospective judgment is empirically myopic and misses the mark.** Electorates systematically weigh the last months more strongly than the previous term and punish the government for events it has nothing to do with - droughts and even shark attacks (classic results of Aken and Bartels). This is not about voter stupidity: attribution in a complex system is objectively difficult, and everyone does it poorly.

**4. The argument proves too much.** If “people learn from the consequences” is enough, then there is no need for bank regulations, or drug certification, or building codes, or traffic rules - all mistakes will be corrected over time by training. We know that this is not so, and we know the cost of finding out. The question is not whether learning occurs, but whether it occurs **faster than the damage accumulates**. For solutions with a 20-year feedback cycle, the answer is arithmetic.

## “Education is constantly adapting to history”

Two objections, and the second one is difficult.

**First.** Who writes the program? Who decides what the lesson of history is? This is again the power of the agenda (M11): the one who determines what history has proven determines the decisions of citizens on all issues at once. It is the most powerful organ possible and is not recognized as an organ.

**Second, more serious.** “People will become educated enough to make the system work” is the load-bearing support of the structure. But if the system works only with a certain quality of the population, then the real subject of design is not the economy, but the education system, and it is this that needs to be described first of all: who teaches what, who checks what happens to those who have not learned. In the history of utopian projects, “we will educate a new person” is exactly the assumption that is the first to fail, and it fails not because people are bad, but because education is never under the control of the designer.

Formally, this makes your concept conditional: it does not “work”, but “works given X,” where X is a strong, untested assumption external to the design itself. Such an assumption cannot be used in response to criticism of the mechanism, because it is itself the subject of criticism.

#IV. "Responsibility lies with the people, not with politicians"

Your premise is correct: the accountability of politicians today is weak. They leave for another position, the damage appears after their term, the party is renamed. This is a real failure and it motivates your entire design legitimately.

But the proposed replacement does not strengthen responsibility, but abolishes it as a working concept. Responsibility requires three things at once:

1. **identifiability** - it is known who decided;
2. **separability** - this someone can be removed;
3. **counterfactual** - in his place, someone else would have decided differently.

In a decision made by forty million people, none of the three exist. There is no one to name, no one to remove, no one to replace. **Responsibility distributed to everyone is the absence of responsibility, not its democratization.** “We are all to blame” is operationally identical to “no one is to blame”: not a single action follows from this phrase.

**Compare two scenarios.** The minister allocated 10 billion to a failed project: resignation, investigation, criminal case, defeat of the party are possible, and - most importantly - the next minister sees the fate of the previous one. Forty million citizens sent 250 units each to the same failed project: no one has consequences, behavior does not change, and nothing prevents it from happening again next month.

**And the same law as in file 07.** The concentration of responsibility on politics is not a defect of the representative system, but its function. Responsibility is concentrated **specifically** so that it becomes effective, just as a residual claimant is appointed specifically so that the loss concerns someone. You eliminate concentration and get not “responsibility from people”, but its evaporation.

**Honest conclusion.** The correct answer to the weak accountability of politicians is to strengthen the liability of identifiable persons: statutes of limitations, personal property liability, mandatory publication of forecasts and subsequent verification of facts, independent investigations, a ban on moving into the industry after regulation. This is all about strengthening the institution, not its abolition. Your design goes in the opposite direction and therefore solves the opposite problem.

# V. What follows from this for the concept

Your four objections produced three different results, and this is worth recording honestly:

- **About the criteria - you are right, and file 07 was incomplete without this part.** The “7/8/5/3” score measures mechanical performance and does not measure fairness. Three ✅ can outweigh all ⚠️ for a person with certain values, and this is a legitimate position, not a mistake.
- **About prices - you are right in the literal sense and wrong in essence.** There will be no inflation; the discrepancy between demands and goods will remain, take the form of scarcity and overhang, and will lose the only quick public signal of its existence.
- **About self-regulation - the objection turned into increased criticism.** Trying to show that the system is learning, you brought to the surface the purest irreducible argument: **the selection of allocators is the accumulation of capital, therefore a system that prohibits accumulation is deprived of a learning mechanism at its level.** This point should be added to file 03 as U13 and put in first place in posts - it is stronger than everything else, because it does not require controversial assumptions and is not repaired setting.
- **About responsibility - the objection does not pass.** Distributed responsibility is operationally equal to zero; concentration of responsibility was a feature, not a defect.
