# Concept, Version 2

This is the refined description of the author's position after the discussion. It is not a critique; objections are kept separately in [15-critique-triage.en.md](15-critique-triage.en.md).

The ⚠️ mark identifies a design choice that has not been fully settled or a remaining internal tension.

## 0. Purpose of the system

**Not accumulation or capital growth.** The goal is to keep society functioning and enable its development.

Growth is not the objective function. The objective is for the system to work and to improve according to the people living in it. Standard measures such as growth rate, capital allocation efficiency, and returns therefore apply only partially.

## 1. Premises

- high labour productivity and automation;
- unconditional provision of basic needs;
- means of production belong to society and cannot be privately owned;
- there is no centralized planning body deciding what and how much to produce.

## 2. Individual share

Every citizen regularly receives an individual share of the social product — a social dividend.

The unused part accumulates in a personal account. Accumulation does **not** grant ownership of factories, land, or other means of production. The citizen may nevertheless direct the accumulated share independently.

⚠️ Open details include inheritance, transfers to another person, what happens on emigration, and when a newcomer starts receiving the dividend.

## 3. Prices

Prices are not tools for extracting profit. For each good and resource, the transparent cost of extraction or production is published. It changes with resource availability and technology. If titanium becomes scarcer, the cost of the next batch rises. Scarce-resource requests show which projects compete for the same physical quantity.

Cost must not rise arbitrarily because of a single market order: physical stocks, production capacity, and alternative projects are considered. The method for calculating full opportunity cost remains to be defined.

## 4. Project funding

Any person or group may propose a project with a stated cost and work plan. Funding is released in tranches rather than as one large transfer, based on progress reporting. A citizen may join with resources or labour.

There is no duty to invest. Since profit is not private income, funds are directed to a project only when it interests the person directing them.

⚠️ The default between tranches is not settled: continue unless cancelled, or stop unless confirmed. Both choices have serious consequences for long-term projects.

## 5. Ordinary production

The state does not set physical quotas such as “produce one million kettles.” If people stop directing resources and labour to kettles, production shrinks. If a shortage appears and resources flow back, production grows.

Transparency replaces external control here: production that nobody wants is visible to everyone, including the workers, and nobody has a personal reason to maintain meaningless output.

## 6. Long-term projects

Basic research, medicines, energy, major infrastructure, and other projects with multi-year horizons cannot be judged by current demand alone. Citizens may direct part of their share to a project for several years. Funding is reviewed as new information appears and stops when failure becomes clear.

## 7. Strategic reserves

Some things are needed before demand appears: specific medicines and medical equipment should exist before an epidemic, not after it. Society sets reserve priorities and sizes and can change them.

Experts and AI provide probabilities, quantities, costs, production times, consequences, historical comparisons, and arguments. They do not have final decision rights.

## 8. Transparency

Every resource-allocation decision must be traceable through: citizens' decision → active rule → source data → calculation → allocation.

There should be no unaccountable closed body. System rules change only by citizens' decision.

## 9. Political structure

The system is maximally decentralized and self-governing. Citizens do not hand over all control to a professional political class.

People are not assumed to be rational at all times. The assumption is that mistakes become visible, decisions can be corrected, and lessons can be learned. Education, access to information, and AI assistants that check claims, calculations, and manipulation are therefore critical.

## 10. Army

A decentralized society still needs organized force against external attack and armed groups. The army is voluntary and professional. It acts without prior voting during a direct armed attack, because defence would otherwise be too slow.

Limits include civilian control, transparent funding, no independent economic base, political neutrality, a predefined limited mandate, accountability for abuse, and no long-term social isolation of military personnel.

The ability to apply force quickly may be centralized, but not the economy, ownership, political legitimacy, and resources at the same time.

## 11. No organized human coercion as the maintenance principle

Maintenance and development should be supported without coercion organized by people: no mandatory levies, assigned tasks, or organ deciding for others.

The justification is not that nobody may use force, but that nobody has a prior exclusive claim to common productive assets. A factory cannot be built alone; land is not produced by anyone; and knowledge is cumulative. The position draws on arguments associated with Herbert Simon, Rawls, and Henry George.

The historical criticism of this foundation is preserved in [16-foundation-of-ownership.en.md](16-foundation-of-ownership.en.md). The current model resolves conflicts over use through citizens' majority decisions and accepts the need for an accountable executor.

## 12. Philosophical basis

People are not expected to stop wanting more. They will create, invent, compete, seek recognition, enjoy work, seek influence, and implement their ideas.

The difference is that the desire for more need not require endless accumulation of private capital.

Capitalist model: accumulate capital → control resources → capital creates more capital.

Proposed model: receive a social dividend → accumulate a share → support an interesting project → the project creates a social result → society becomes richer.

## 13. Operational coordination and scarce resources

Workers run existing enterprises through a chosen organizer or management and may invite outside specialists. A factory need not continue without demand; it closes or changes profile when demand disappears.

For resources requested by several projects, each request states quantity, quality, and a consumption calendar. Producers publish capacity forecasts, stocks, and confirmed deliveries. The system shows citizens the alternatives: which project receives the resource and which projects are delayed. Support gives a conditional reservation, not ownership; reservations are periodically confirmed. Citizens may also approve priorities and reserves for basic consumption and maintenance.

Irreversible projects publish risk estimates, maximum acceptable loss, responsible decision-makers, and a disposal or completion plan. Transparency includes public decision logs and audits, not necessarily operational police or intelligence data.

## 14. Personal property and housing

Personal belongings and household tools may be personal property as long as they are not used to hire labour or extract rent. Land remains common, while a building may be personally held under limited exchange and no rent extraction. Primary housing provides a protected right of residence and is allocated according to actual need. A dacha is a seasonal right to use a common plot and building. The full proposal is in [29-personal-property-and-housing.en.md](29-personal-property-and-housing.en.md).

New housing or another major object is built only with sufficient support from future users or other citizens. Every six months an automated system publishes use data; any citizen may initiate a vote to transfer an underused resource to a more needed user. The new user pays reasonable relocation costs. A rotating enforcement mechanism and parallel audit apply if an organization refuses to implement the decision.

## Appendix: changes from version 1

- the goal is maintenance and development rather than capital accumulation;
- prices reflect transparent changing production costs rather than fixed nominal prices;
- project funding is staged and investing is not mandatory;
- transparency replaces external control for ordinary production;
- the tension around the absence of organized coercion is explicit;
- unresolved design choices are marked rather than hidden.
