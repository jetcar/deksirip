# Part 10. Can transparency replace selection

Objection: the system is transparent; if they make bearings that no one takes, then they will even stop investing their own, because there is no more personal income - either it’s a benefit to everyone, or it’s a pointless exercise. Plus: people still invest in something that brings nothing (streamers).

The analysis below gives different results for different classes of solutions, so the conclusion is not “cancelled” and not “remained”, but **narrows down to a specific area**.

---

# I. Where you are completely right: bearings

For an ordinary repeatable product with observable demand, transparency is **sufficient**, and no capital market is needed for this.

Why does the signal work here:
- **cheap** — unsold warehouse is visible without analysis;
- **unambiguous** - there is one metric, there is nothing to argue about;
- **fast** - feedback cycle of weeks, maximum months;
- **attribute** - it’s clear what decision led to this.

With these four properties, the selection of allocators is truly redundant: any participant, including the most interested ones, sees the same thing and cannot in good faith assert the opposite.

Moreover, your argument about the disappearance of personal income is also correct here and points to a real mechanism. Zombie companies in capitalism survive largely not in spite of discipline, but **because of the private benefits of maintaining them**: the bank does not want to recognize the loss on the loan and extends it (a classic Japanese practice of the 1990s, described in the literature on zombie lending), the venture capital fund does not want to write off the valuation in its statements, management defends its positions. Take away these participants and this particular channel of failure survivability truly disappears.

**This should be written down as an asset of the concept.** For the segment of ordinary consumer goods - and this is the majority of the economy by volume - the stated mechanism “production follows where people direct resources” is workable, and transparency enhances it.

But note that it is in this segment that the concept is least original: “they make what they charge” is a description of the general consumer goods market. The novelty of the concept is concentrated elsewhere - in the financing of new projects. There are four properties of the signal, none of them are satisfied.

---

#II. Where transparency doesn't give a signal to stop

## 1. A project whose result should not yet exist has no unsold stock

Thermonuclear project in its seventh year: 8 billion spent, no results. Absolutely transparent. And it is completely uninformative, because **this picture is identical for a project going according to plan and for a hopeless one**.

Transparency gives data. She doesn't give an interpretation. The debate about whether to proceed with ITER is not a debate about the facts, the facts are public; it is a dispute about the meaning of facts. Full transparency does not resolve it and does not bring it closer to resolution.

## 2. When there are several metrics, transparency multiplies the arguments rather than reducing them

The bearing has one metric, and it is not disputed. For educational reform, for a drug in the second phase, for urban development, for a research program, there are dozens of metrics, all measurable, all published, and they point in different directions. Each side honestly refers to real data.

Plus, Goodhart's law kicks in immediately: as soon as a specific number determines funding, the project begins to optimize that number. Transparency makes the target number public and therefore **faster** optimizable.

## 3. Counterfactual comparison is not available in principle

This is the deepest of the three. Any decision on the allocation of resources is compared with an unrealized alternative: was it worth spending 10 billion here, and not in three other projects? Transparency shows what happened. Data about what would have happened does not exist **anywhere** - not because they are hidden, but because they do not exist.

No level of openness creates information about the road not taken. This is why price, for all its flaws, does what transparency cannot: it compresses the judgments of many people about what did not happen into a single number.

---

#III. Does the motive to support failure disappear along with personal gain?

Your thesis: without personal income, there is no one to defend a failed project. Let's check who is really interested in continuing.

**Leaving** (and this is a real gain): bank deferment of loss recognition, maintaining estimates in the fund’s statements, withdrawing money through friendly contracts.

**Remains and intensifies:**

**1. Busyness - and here the asymmetry turns against you.**
The project employs four thousand people who joined through labor. Stopping means they need to look for something else to do. Who's interested in stopping? Citizens, each of whom loses 250 units and does not notice it.

Now use Olson. **In capitalism, the decision to “stop” is made by a concentrated interest - the owner losing money - against a dispersed one: workers, suppliers, the city.** That is why the stop occurs, although it is always unpopular. In your system, "stop" requires a dispersed, unorganized mass to actively withdraw funding, against a concentrated, organized and motivated group that wants to continue.

Olson says who wins such clashes. You didn't eliminate the problem of the persistence of failures - you eliminated **the only participant who had both the motive and the ability to stop**.

**2. Transparency creates a public commitment trap.**
If the register forever shows that I financed this project, then the admission of failure is a public admission of my mistake, recorded indelibly. The rational response is to keep funding so that the project ultimately succeeds and the record looks right.

This is an escalation of obligations, and in studies of this effect, the key factor turns out to be personal responsibility for the initial decision, and publicity enhances it. Your full transparency is not a neutral good: it maximizes exactly the factor that creates escalation.

**3. The status has not disappeared - it has been declared the main incentive.**
The philosophical core of the concept directly preserves the desire for recognition, influence, and implementation of one’s ideas. This means that status is a private benefit. And it is **worse** than money in this regard: money is diversified, status is not. A project manager whose reputation is identical to the project loses more when stopped than a venture investor with a portfolio of forty companies. He will resist more.

**Section summary:** Removing monetary benefits removes some channels of failure survivability and strengthens others. The direction of the total effect is not in your favor, because the structure of the coalitions changes: the “continue” side becomes concentrated, the “stop” side becomes dispersed.

---

#IV. Streamers: a good example, but it proves the wrong thing

You yourself doubted the example - let me clarify what exactly.

**Why this is not a counterexample to selection.** Paying a streamer is **consumption**, not an investment. A person pays and immediately receives what he came for, just like for a concert. No one expected a refund, the transaction was completed, there was simply no point in placing resources incorrectly here. This has nothing to do with the issue of capital.

About laundering: a hypothesis is not needed, the income of top streamers is explained by ordinary subscriptions, advertising and sponsorship.

**Why this example is valuable - it works both ways.**

*For the concept:* this is direct evidence that the voluntary allocation of funds without any return mobilizes real, big money. The “no one will volunteer to fund” objection has been empirically refuted, and you can refer to it.

*Cons:* This is a working model of your funding mechanism in miniature, and its observable result is an extremely power-law distribution, where attention, personality, and network effects are everything, and there is no connection to any measure of public benefit. A few huge winners and a long tail of nothing.

That is, streamers are the answer to the question “will voluntary funding work?” (yes) and at the same time the answer to the question “on what basis will it distribute resources?” (by attention and charm).

---

# V. Result: the clause is not canceled, but narrowed a second time

| Solution class | Is transparency enough? Why |
|---|---|---|
| Regular replicable products with observable demand | **Yes** | The signal is cheap, unambiguous, fast, attributable |
| Improvement of existing production | Most likely yes | Comparison with the observed basis is possible |
| New product with short cycle | Partially | The metric appears in a year or two, the continuation coalition has already formed |
| Project with a horizon of 10–20 years | **No** | “A lot spent, no result” is identical for success and failure |
| Projects with controversial metrics | **No** | Transparency multiplies arguments, Goodhart gets involved faster |
| Any comparison with an unrealized alternative | **No** | Counterfactuals do not exist anywhere |

**New wording U13, third edition:**

> For conventional repeatable products, transparency replaces selection completely, and in this segment the concept is workable. For projects whose outcome does not yet exist, whose metric is disputed, or whose evaluation requires comparison with a failed alternative, transparency provides data but does not provide a stop signal. In this area, the removal of monetary benefits does not eliminate the persistence of failures: it removes some channels and strengthens the rest, and most importantly, it inverts the structure of coalitions, making the “continue” side concentrated and organized, and the “stop” side dispersed.

**What does this mean for the concept itself?** Pay attention to the pattern: the mechanism turns out to be more reliable, the less original it is. Where the concept boils down to "they make what they take", it works - and coincides with the general consumer market. Where it claims to be new—a citizen directs savings to a 10 billion project with a twenty-year horizon—none of the conditions for operability are met.

This is not a verdict, but it is an indication of where exactly the limit of applicability lies, and it runs across the main declared mechanism, and not along the edge of the structure.
