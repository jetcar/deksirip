## B. Historical and modern counterexamples (what has already been tried)

### B1. Yugoslav workers' self-government (1950–1990)
The immediate realization of “the means of production are public, there is no plan.”
Result: Ward-Domar-Vanek effect, horizon problem, chronic unemployment with labor shortages in unpopular industries, inflation, regional divergence, collapse. **Directly relevant to your M7.**

### B2. Coupon privatization (Czech Republic, Russia, 1990s)
A real experiment in distributing non-redeemable shares to the entire population. Result: rapid concentration through intermediary funds, because dispersed holders do not monitor and sell. **Directly relevant M3, M4, M9.**

### B3. Participatory Budget, Porto Alegre (1989–2017)
The most successful case of direct distribution of resources by citizens. There are real achievements (water supply, sewerage in poor areas). But: participation of 1-2% of adults, dominance of organized associations, outreach is part of capital budget, not economics; the program was curtailed after the change of government. **Relevant M12, M13.**

### B4. Kibbutzim (Israel, 1910s - 2000s)
Equal sharing, common property, voluntariness. Lasted for generations. Privatized in the 1990s–2000s. The works of Ran Abramitsky show adverse selection: the most productive leave, the “price of ideology” grows with the growth of external options. **Relevant M17, U8.**

### B5. Gitcoin Grants and quadratic funding (2019–)
Regular rounds of matching funding for public goods. Each round - detection of collusion and sibyls, manual disqualifications, permanent anti-fraud team, experiments with identity systems. **Relevant M6, M12.**

### B6. DAO governance (2016–)
The DAO (2016) is a hack and a hard fork, that is, the final decision was made by a narrow circle of developers, and not by the “community”. Further: persistently low participation (1–10%), concentration of delegates, “governance mining”, attacks on the treasury through the purchase of votes (for example, the story of Build Finance DAO - capture through voting). **Relevant M4, M13, M19.**

### B7. ICO boom 2017 and crowdfunding
Direct financing of projects by the general public without a professional filter. According to Satis Group, about 78% of projects that have raised funds are scams or dead. Kickstarter: A significant percentage of funded projects do not deliver as promised. **Relevant M5.**

###B8. Science crowdfunding
Experiment.com and similar. Median amounts are thousands of dollars, thematic bias towards charming topics. No fundamental program has ever been funded like this. **Relevant to question 5.**

###B9. Pandemic reserves until 2020
Most developed countries had plans and specialized bodies; Adequate supplies of PPE and capacity were almost nowhere to be found. Shows that even a body with a mandate and budget systematically underfunds rare catastrophic risks. The mechanism of voluntary attention of citizens will cope worse. **Relevant to question 7.**

###B10. Mobilization economies (USA 1942, Britain 1939–45)
War Production Board, Emergency Powers (Defense) Act. Directive reallocation of a huge share of output over months. No voluntary scheme has replicated this. Plus ratchet powers after the war. **Relevant M16.**

###B11. Alaska Permanent Fund Dividend (1982–)
The only long-lived unconditional dividend financed by public ownership of a resource. Works due to its small size, external resource rent and statutory formula. After 2016, the size of the payments became a political issue - Figure M8. **Relevant to question 14.**

### B12. Non-convertible parallel currencies and card systems
Berezka checks in the USSR, Cuban CUC, wartime card systems. Everywhere, secondary markets and exchange rates emerged over months, and the fight against them required a repressive apparatus. **Relevant M9.**

### B13. LiquidFeedback and the German Pirate Party (2010–2013)
A real attempt at liquid democracy. The problem of overdelegates, low participation, tool abandonment. **Relevant M4, M13.**

###B14. Cybersyn (Chile, 1971–73) and OGAS (USSR)
Attempts to technologically solve the coordination problem. Cybersyn terminated by coup; OGAS is blocked by departmental interests. Indicative of question 13 and the “AI will help” topic.

###B15. Spanish Mondragon
The largest operating cooperative conglomerate. Important: he **has** a bank (Caja Laboral), a central apparatus, pay differentiation (historically the ceiling of the ratio is about 1:6, later higher), and he bankrupts his enterprises (Fagor Electrodomésticos, 2013). That is, a functioning cooperative sector exists - but with precisely those institutions that your concept prohibits.
