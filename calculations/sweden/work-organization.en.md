# Self-regulation of the working week

## Necessary result, not time spent at work

The model should not require people to fill a predetermined number of hours. First define the required output and quality; then participants distribute the work needed to provide it.

Possible schedules include 20 hours in four short shifts, 30 hours in five six-hour shifts, long or irregular shifts where continuity matters, and project work with periods of complete absence.

```text
effective_hours = scheduled_hours × productivity_factor
```

The productivity factor depends on shift length, fatigue, handovers, switching, and process continuity. It differs between doctors, programmers, miners, and grid operators.

Current S3 estimates of 1.49–3.32 million FTE are normalized to 40 hours and a productivity factor of 1. They do not mean that this many people must work full-time. For the central 2.215 million FTE, the linear estimates are 2.215 million participants at 40 hours, 2.953 million at 30 hours, and 4.43 million at 20 hours.

Citizens do not have to occupy a job or fill a quota of hours. Society publishes required results; participants choose tasks, schedules, and shares. If too few people participate, bonuses rise, the process is simplified, or automation accelerates.

Measure output, quality and errors, actual person-hours, and resilience to worker absence — not employment as such.
