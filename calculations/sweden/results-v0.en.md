# S3-v1: recalculation after separating medicine from care and digitizing education

## Result

Under the assumptions in `scenario-s3-v0.csv`, necessary human labour is estimated at:

| Scenario | FTE | Of total population | Of current employed age 15–74 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 1.49 million | 14.0% | 28.3% |
| Central | 2.215 million | 20.9% | 42.1% |
| High | 3.32 million | 31.3% | 63.1% |

FTE means full-time equivalent at 40 hours per week. Sweden's population is 10.605 million and current employment age 15–74 is 5.263 million.

| Average week | Low | Central | High |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 40 hours | 1.49m / 14.0% | 2.215m / 20.9% | 3.32m / 31.3% |
| 30 hours | 1.987m / 18.7% | 2.953m / 27.8% | 4.427m / 41.7% |
| 20 hours | 2.98m / 28.1% | 4.43m / 41.8% | 6.64m / 62.6% |

The 30- and 20-hour rows are linear conversions, not additional labour. Shorter schedules may improve hourly productivity or increase handover losses; the final model must use task-specific effective hours. See [work-organization.en.md](work-organization.en.md).

## What 20 hours means compared with Sweden today

Twenty hours is half of a nominal 40-hour week. Sweden's observed average is already below 40 hours, so moving to 20 hours is not literally a 50% reduction from current average work. In the central scenario, 4.43 million people at 20 hours would participate, compared with 2.215 million full-time equivalents and about 5.263 million currently employed people.

The number of participants falls sharply only when automation and productivity reduce the necessary human hours themselves.

## Status and next step

This is a scenario estimate, not a statistical result. The FTE values are assumptions used to show order of magnitude and sensitivity. Medicine, care, construction, and repair require the most careful validation.
